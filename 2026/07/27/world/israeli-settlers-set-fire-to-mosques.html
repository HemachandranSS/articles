MIDDLE EAST · WEST BANK CONFLIC

Israeli settlers set fire to mosques in West Bank following deadly clashes

Incident escalated after a settler group descended on the Palestinian village; local people say graffiti in mosque called for revenge; Israeli military says troops searching for suspects; four Palestinians and two Israelis killed in Friday’s shooting

REUTERS | NABLUS

Israeli settlers attacked Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank early on Sunday, setting fire to two mosques and scrawling graffiti on buildings, Palestinian officials said.

The attacks followed an incident on Friday when four Palestinians and two Israeli soldiers were killed when a crowd of Israeli settlers approached the Palestinian village of Tal, south-west of Nablus. During the incident, villagers came out of their homes to confront the settlers and a stand-off ensued.

Palestinian local authorities say the settlers provoked the violence. The Israeli military said shots were fired by both sides.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, facing an election in October, ordered a security operation against what he described as terrorism against Israeli civilians, reinforcing units in the West Bank.

In an apparent reaction to the deaths of the Israelis, settlers carried out a series of attacks on Palestinian villages in West Bank.

Abdel Azim Wadi, head of the village council in Qusra, said settlers had torched a newly completed mosque in his village early on Sunday. “They set it on fire and spray-painted Hebrew slogans on its walls,” he said. Among the graffiti were messages including “Jewish revenge”.

“Israeli state-backed settler terrorism is intensifying into systematic pogroms across the occupied West Bank.”

‘SYSTEMATIC POGROMS’

The Palestinian Authority, which has responsibility for the section of the West Bank where Friday’s shootings took place, said the incident was part of a generalised assault on Palestinians. “Israeli state-backed settler terrorism is intensifying into systematic pogroms across the occupied West Bank,” the Palestinian Prime Minister’s office said.

The Israeli military said troops sent to Qusra were searching for suspects.

In separate incidents, Palestinian officials said settlers had also tried to burn a mosque near Kour. Farid Jiyousi, a member of the Kour village council, said worshippers had put out the fire before it reached the main area of the mosque.

Most countries and UN consider Israeli settlements in the West Bank illegal, a position Israel rejects.
