France, Spain battle ‘unpredictable’ wildfires as tens of thousands flee

Massive blazes driven by erratic winds and summer heat force hundreds of thousands of evacuations across major tourist and regional hubs in France and Spain.

By Agence France-Presse | BORDEAUX

Field reporting from BORDEAUX via Agence France-Presse (AFP) news dispatch on the European summer wildfire emergency.

France fought an “unpredictable” wildfire close to Bordeaux on Sunday while Spain warned of “difficult hours” in its own fiery battle, with evacuations from danger zones in both countries mounting to well over 300,000 people.

France’s major fire remained unchecked and volatile – driven by gusts that in some cases were generated by the turbulent blazes themselves. Spanish officials, however, voiced optimism they were starting to get a grip on some fires – though on the weekend they were confronted with a new fire front, near Valencia, which forced 15,000 people to flee.

Around 75,000 residents have been evacuated from the Madrid region, Avila, Toledo and from the eastern region of Castellon in Valencia.

“Difficult hours lie ahead admittedly,” Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said as he visited the fire zone in Avila.

Scientists say that such weather emergencies are being made more extreme and frequent by climate change. The disasters came at the peak of the summer vacation period when tourists flock to both countries.

France’s Interior Minister Laurent Nunez warned “the situation remains very unfavourable” for fires across the country, where 250,000 people had been evacuated.

“The main fire in the southwest Gironde region... was ‘extremely intense and unpredictable... generating its own winds and advancing erratically’.”
