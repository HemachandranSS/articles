Women’s quota should be implemented without delimitation: ex-HC judge

Speakers at the launch of 'The Delimitation Debate: The Union and its Units' argue that freezing seat reassignment and unlinking political reservation from Census delays is crucial to safeguard federal balance.

BY THE HINDU BUREAU

CHENNAI  |  Constitutional Governance, Federalism & Electoral Reforms

The freeze on delimitation of Lok Sabha constituencies should continue and women’s reservation should be implemented immediately after being delinked from delimitation and the Census, the former Madras High Court judge D. Hariparanthaman (retd) said on Thursday.

Releasing the book The Delimitation Debate: The Union and its Units, published by The Hindu Group here, the former judge cautioned that an increase in the number of Lok Sabha seats would hamper meaningful deliberations in the House. “The problem of raising the numbers is that deliberations cannot take place,” he said.

Besides, if the increase is based on population, southern States will stand to lose adequate representation in the Lok Sabha, he said. This will amount to penalising the southern States for having implemented the national population policy well. “Taking into consideration other democracies, 543 [the current strength of the Lok Sabha] itself is a good number,” he said.

The former judge batted for equitable representation of all States in the Rajya Sabha, much like the U.S. Senate model.

K. Ashok Vardhan Shetty, retired IAS officer and member of the High-level Committee on Centre-State Relations constituted by the Tamil Nadu government, said Article 329(a) of the Constitution placed the Delimitation Commission’s proposals inside a constitutional “black box”, insulating it from executive, parliamentary, and judicial scrutiny.

The commission comprises a retired Supreme Court judge as chairperson, the Chief Election Commissioner or a nominee, and the State Election Commissioner of the State concerned. “This is dangerous and has to be corrected. In Japan and Germany, the delimitation commission advises but the legislatures pass the delimitation legislation,” he said.

Mr. Shetty suggested the introduction of the “dialogue model” implemented in Canada where the delimitation commission’s proposals are deliberated by a parliamentary sub-committee and the remarks are sent back to the commission for clarification. To improve representation in both Houses, the Rajya Sabha, or the House of States, may be recast on the lines of the U.S. Senate, while for the Lok Sabha, a digressive proportionality system, as in practice in Brazil, may be implemented to reduce the gap between the more populous States and the smaller ones. Delimitation should be frozen until all States fell within a narrow band of the National Total Fertility Rate, he said.

N. Ram, Director, The Hindu Group Publishing Pvt. Ltd., suggested that the ideas discussed in the book be distilled to make them accessible for all, which then may be translated to other languages.

Nirmala Lakshman, Publisher, The Hindu Group, and Suresh Nambath, Editor, The Hindu, were present. R. Srinivasan, head, Magazines and Special Publications, THG Publishing, said the title was the Group’s 219th publication since the formation of a Special Publications vertical in 2017.

The book is a collection of 17 essays — preceded by an introduction — on delimitation, federalism, representation, and Census.
