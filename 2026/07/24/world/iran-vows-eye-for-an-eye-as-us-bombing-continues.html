Iran vows ‘eye for an eye’ as U.S. bombing continues

Tehran and its regional allies scale up retaliation across key maritime routes and infrastructure as U.S. strikes hit Iranian sites for the 12th consecutive night.

BY AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE

TEHRAN, WASHINGTON  |  Geopolitics, Maritime Security & Regional Conflict

Iran vowed on Thursday to keep striking the region so long as it remains under attack, as the war spread to new fronts with Tehran’s Houthi allies attacking Saudi shipping in the Red Sea.

The Houthis of Yemen this week joined the conflict, claiming to have struck two Saudi oil tankers after declaring a blockade of Saudi ports. Reacting, U.S. President Donald Trump threatened Iran and the Houthis with “major military punishment”.

Meanwhile, Jordan and Kuwait both reported intercepting strikes as Iran’s military and Revolutionary Guards claimed to be targeting U.S. assets in both countries. “The armed forces’ retaliatory attacks will continue as long as the U.S. attacks on the country’s infrastructure and coastal areas continue,” Iran’s army spokesperson Mohammad Akraminia said, according to state TV.

The developments followed new U.S. strikes on Iranian military targets, with U.S. Central Command reporting its 12th consecutive night of strikes, saying it aimed to “further degrade Iran’s ability to threaten civilian mariners and commercial vessels transiting regional waters”.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran would respond in kind to any attack on its infrastructure, writing: “Our defence doctrine is clear: eye for an eye.”

Iranian state media said on Thursday that a U.S. attack killed two people at Shalamcheh on the Islamic Republic’s border with Iraq. It also reported two U.S. missiles hit Bushehr, home to the country’s only civilian nuclear plant.

The Houthis said they had carried out missile and drone strikes on two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea, the Encelia and the Layla, after declaring a blockade of Saudi ports.

Saudi Arabia confirmed the Encelia had been struck, without commenting on the other tanker.

In Hormuz, the Revolutionary Guards said they stopped three oil tankers from transiting, as the U.S. and Iran vie for control over the route.

“The Strait of Hormuz is under our control... and any ship that is deceived by the U.S. and intends to pass without coordination with the Islamic Republic of Iran will suffer the same fate,” the Revolutionary Guards said.

In recent weeks, Iran has hit tankers using a route hugging the Omani coast to traverse the strait, in a bid to strong-arm vessels into using its own waters along its coast and pay for safe passage.
