U.S. House approves $95-bn budget, most of it for Iran war

A narrow 216-214 vote sends a massive military and election funding framework to the Senate ahead of crucial midterm elections.

BY AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE

WASHINGTON  |  Defense Appropriations, Legislative Strategy & Geopolitical Finance

The U.S. House of Representatives approved a 95-billion budget framework on Wednesday, with most of the funding earmarked for the Pentagon to fund the war against Iran.

The measure was narrowly approved in a 216-214 vote and now heads to the Senate, where its fate is uncertain.

The plan provides 73 billion for the armed forces and intelligence agencies, notably to fund operations linked to the war against Iran, as well as 12 billion in aid for farmers affected by President Donald Trump’s trade war.

An additional 10 billion would go towards election-related programmes as part of Mr. Trump’s push to enact new nationwide voting restrictions while his broader “SAVE America Act” lacks enough support to proceed.

The budget package represents the Republican party’s last major legislative push before November’s midterm elections, the outcome of which will determine whether the party retains its majority in Congress and thus continues to shape the remainder of Mr. Trump’s second term.

Democratic Representative Rosa DeLauro denounced a measure that would allow Mr. Trump to finance the continuation of his war against Iran.
