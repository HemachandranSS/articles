4 hybrid funds to consider this Deepavali

Middle path — diversified strategies to manage volatility and cushion market declines without missing the next equity rally

Dhuraivel Gunasekaran, Kumar Shankar Roy

BL Research Bureau  |  Sunday, November 8, 2026

Each Samvat brings its own lessons; Samvat 2082 underlines asset allocation. From tariff wars and West Asian conflict to crude shocks, record FPI selling and AI-led disruption, Indian equities have faced serial stress tests. Hybrid funds offer a middle path between waiting for “better prices” and going all-in on equities.

We shortlist four schemes across aggressive hybrid, balanced advantage, equity savings and multi-asset categories — screened on cycle performance, risk-adjusted returns, downside protection, portfolio composition, process and return consistency. Equity savings, balanced advantage and multi-asset funds suit lump-sum as well as SIP; aggressive hybrid funds, with higher unhedged equity, favour SIPs or staggered lumpsums in the present environment.

Kotak Aggressive Hybrid Fund — staying the course in a changing market

For moderate-risk investors wary of volatility, aggressive hybrid funds (65–80% equity) cushion drawdowns while retaining upside. The ₹9,040-crore Kotak Aggressive Hybrid Fund (direct plan, seven-year CAGR 15.2% vs category 13.4%) keeps equity exposure between 69% and 80% — currently 75%. It favours cash-generating businesses with visible three-to-four-year earnings growth, avoiding unrelated diversification and dilution. As of September 2026, 39% of equity was large-cap and 33% mid/small-cap — a relatively high mid-cap tilt. Top sectors: banks, financial services, retail. Debt uses active duration (Macaulay 3.3 years; YTM 6.6% vs category 7.3%); credit risk is minimal (~1.3% AA). Five-year rolling returns averaged 17.8% (category 16%); direct-plan expense ratio 0.45%.

Mirae Asset Equity Savings Fund — a measured approach to wealth creation

Blending unhedged equity, arbitrage and quality debt, the ₹1,994-crore scheme held 38.7% unhedged domestic equity, 28.2% hedged equity, 18% corporate debt, 9.7% G-Secs and 5.5% cash (September 2026). Net equity has stayed in a 36–45% band, adjusted via P/E and P/B vs long-term averages. Mean five-year rolling return 12.03% (min 8.29%, max 17.04%); seven-year SIP 10.23% (category 8.74%). Large-cap bias limits volatility; debt favours G-Secs and high-grade corporates. Equity taxation applies; direct expense ratio 0.31%.

ICICI Prudential Balanced Advantage Fund — a model-led de-risking strategy

The ₹71,913-crore category leader (direct plan, 11.8% annualised over seven years vs 10.8%) runs a counter-cyclical model: daily net equity recommendations from valuations (especially P/B), rates, FX and crude, combined with stock picking and derivatives. Historical net equity band 30–80%; gross equity stays above 65% for tax treatment, with futures, puts and covered calls when net exposure falls. Portfolio is large-cap heavy; overweight autos, retail, internet; underweight cement, oil & gas, BFSI. Debt duration 4.1 years; YTM 7.5%. Five-year rolling average 13.7% (category 12.4%); direct expense ratio 0.71%.

WhiteOak Capital Multi Asset Allocation Fund — diversification beyond equities

Launched May 2023 (AUM ₹9,111 crore), WhiteOak shifts dynamically across domestic equity, debt, gold, REITs, InvITs, overseas equity and commodity derivatives. Latest mix: 27.8% unhedged equity, 22.97% corporate debt, 10.6% gold, 11.1% REITs, 9.5% InvITs, plus G-Secs and cash. Equity tilt ~73% large-cap. Three-year CAGR 16% (category 14.25%); three-year SIP 11.92% (category 8.19%). Minimum horizon five years; direct expense ratio 0.39%.
