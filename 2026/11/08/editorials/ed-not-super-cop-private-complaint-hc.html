ED not a ‘super cop’, can’t file private complaint: HC

ED’s power under PMLA is ‘derivative and not original’, says Karnataka HC; it adds that the agency can’t prosecute an offence on its own when competent agencies decline to act on its information.

Krishnaprasad

BENGALURU  |  November 8, 2026

Declaring the Directorate of Enforcement (ED) is “not a super cop,” the Karnataka High Court held the agency cannot file a private complaint to initiate a money-laundering probe when competent agencies decline to prosecute a scheduled offence based on information supplied by the ED. Justice M.I. Arun allowed writ petitions by Malur MLA K.Y. Nanjegowda and the Malur Land Grant Committee challenging ECIR proceedings and seizures tied to alleged land and job scams at KOMUL (Kolar and Chikkaballapur Milk Union Ltd.).

The court ruled ED’s power under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) is “derivative and not original.” Filing a private complaint under Section 200 of the Code of Criminal Procedure or Section 223 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita was not contemplated under PMLA. If the ED could lodge private complaints on its own, it would investigate beyond what Parliament authorised.

One institution in the country does not become supreme so that it is in a position to abuse the powers conferred on it. Law recognises the requirements of checks and balances. — Karnataka High Court

The order reinforces that scheduled offences and primary investigating agencies remain gatekeepers; the ED’s ECIR cannot substitute for that chain when police or other authorities refuse to act.

Key Terminologies

  1. PMLA Derivative Jurisdiction (PMLA வழித்தோன்றல் அதிகாரம்)
  2. Section 200 CrPC / BNSS 223 (தனியார் வழக்கு விதிகள்)
  3. KOMUL Land and Job Scam (KOMUL மோசடி)
