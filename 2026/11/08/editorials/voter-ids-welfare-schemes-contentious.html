Why is the use of voter IDs for welfare schemes contentious?

What did Panchayati Raj Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh say? Have voter IDs been used before? What are the concerns?

Sobhana K. Nair

Explainer  |  November 8, 2026

On November 3, Union Panchayati Raj Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh said voter cards would replace Aadhaar for welfare payments under schemes his Ministry handles, arguing that Aadhaar is too easily forged. On November 6 he clarified on X that no rightful beneficiary would be excluded and that voter IDs might be used alongside Aadhaar — but the announcement sharpened an already polarised debate.

Are voter IDs already used?

Electoral photo identity cards (pehchaan patra) already serve as entry documents for entitlements such as MGNREGA job cards; some States, including Delhi, require them for pensions. They have not, however, been the exclusive nationwide identifier for welfare delivery. Ten States have introduced “family cards” for household-targeted programmes.

Concerns

Opposition parties link the proposal to the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, where roughly 13 crore electors were removed. Nikhil Dey (Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan) warned that exclusion could operate at two levels — disenfranchisement, then welfare denial. West Bengal’s Suvendu Adhikari has moved to restrict welfare lists to those on electoral rolls.

Delivery implications

The Ministry works through gram sabhas; the 16th Finance Commission (2026–31) recommended grants totalling ₹4,35,236 crore for panchayats. Because identification policy may not apply uniformly across all Central schemes, States and beneficiaries face uncertainty until rules are final.
