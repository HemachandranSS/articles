The progressive’s progress

The Michigan Senator-elect describes himself as ‘just a capitalist who understands how capitalism works’ and identifies as a progressive; his candidacy was backed by Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez

Abdul El-Sayed

Profile by Srinivasan Ramani  |  November 8, 2026

Abdul El-Sayed’s election made him the first Muslim member of the U.S. Senate, defeating Republican Mike Rogers in one of the costliest Senate races in history. A public-health physician and former Detroit health officer, he campaigned on Medicare for All and challenged big-tech data centres over electricity and water use.

Foreign policy

He was outspoken on Israel, calling it a “rogue state” and an “ethno-state,” and pledged to end U.S. military aid to Israel, Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Egypt — positions that energised progressive donors but alarmed centrist Democrats.

Reshaping health policy

Rhodes Scholar at Oxford and Columbia-trained doctor, El-Sayed previously ran for Michigan Governor in 2017. His victory in a state that backed Donald Trump in 2016 and 2024 tests whether progressive platforms can win in the Rust Belt.

Litmus test

Analysts frame the result as weakening the claim that single-payer health care is an electoral liability outside safe Democratic seats — provided the party can hold the coalition he assembled.
