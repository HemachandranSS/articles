What are the changes to the Agnipath scheme?

What has changed for Agniveers? Why has the government introduced these changes? What did the four-year assessment show? How will the changes benefit Agniveers and the armed forces?

Saurabh Trivedi

Explainer  |  November 8, 2026

On November 4, the government announced an overhaul of the Agnipath scheme. Agniveers due to exit after four years will receive a two-year extension, after which they may compete for enrolment in the regular cadre. The 25% retention rule for permanent absorption is unchanged; the remaining 75% may opt into this second engagement with higher remuneration and an enhanced Seva Nidhi package.

Why change now?

Chief of Defence Staff General N.S. Raja Subramani cited a comprehensive assessment after four years of implementation, using a centralised database to track performance across Army, Navy and Air Force. The aim is to retain trained manpower and deepen operational experience.

Assessment findings

Officials highlight wider district recruitment, transparent selection, high fitness and morale, and expanded roles for women across branches.

Second engagement period

Agniveers may serve six years in total before consideration for regular enrolment. Those who choose the extension forgo post-service reservation benefits in CAPFs and State police forces — a trade-off the policy frames as a pathway to permanent service.

For the armed forces

The reform reduces churn in training pipelines and keeps battle-ready personnel in uniform longer without abandoning the short-service model’s fiscal logic.

Key Terminologies

  1. Agniveer Second Engagement (இரண்டாம் பணிநிலை நீட்சி)
  2. 25% Regular Cadre Retention (நிரந்தர படை 25% தக்கவைப்பு)
  3. CDS Four-Year Review (பாதுகாப்புத் தலைவர் மதிப்பீடு)
  4. CAPF Reservation Trade-off (மத்திய படைகள் ஒதுக்கீடு சம்மந்தம்)
