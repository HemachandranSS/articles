INTERNATIONAL RELATIONS · UNITED STATES

Why are the U.S. midterm elections significant?

What do the results tell us about American voters’ attitudes towards President Donald Trump? What do they mean for Democrats and Republicans? What lies ahead? Why do these results matter for the rest of the world?

Narayan Lakshman

Explainer  |  November 8, 2026

Democrats secured control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives in the November 2026 midterms, ending President Donald Trump’s trifecta over the federal government. The party flipped traditional Republican terrain — including Texas, South Carolina and Iowa — while holding swing states such as Georgia and Michigan.

The story so far

The President must now work with Democratic lawmakers on appropriations, legislation and oversight. Democrats gain powers over committee chairs, investigations and confirmation of nominees, though they lack a two-thirds supermajority to override presidential vetoes.

Republican position

Job approval for Mr. Trump remains low; Vice-President J.D. Vance attributed losses on social media partly to the Iran war. Stephen Miller’s appointment as Chief of Staff signals a hardened White House response even as Congress prepares sharper scrutiny.

Issues foregrounded

Oil prices spiked toward $110–$118 amid military action against Iran in early 2026. Tariffs and ICE raids/deportations galvanised progressive turnout. New Hampshire’s Chris Pappas was among Democrats celebrating Senate gains.

Way forward for Democrats

Leaders such as Hakeem Jeffries face pressure for accountability and investigations while avoiding perceived overreach — such as immediate impeachment — that could alienate moderates.

Global impact

Congress can curtail military appropriations, exercise oversight on the Justice Department and ICE, and shape foreign-policy debates — including nuclear adventurism with Iran — even as the President retains veto power over immigration and regulatory rewrites.

On the flip side for the Democrats, the President still holds the right to veto bills passed by Congress, and this limits the extent to which Congress can rewrite laws and regulations in areas such as immigration enforcement.
