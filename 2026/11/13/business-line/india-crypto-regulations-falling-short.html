Are India’s crypto regulations falling short?

Trading on crypto platforms has shifted to futures and options which are not subject to punitive tax

Lokeshwarri SK

The Hindu Business Line  |  November 13, 2026

After the 2020–21 crypto boom that took Bitcoin from about $5,178 to $64,400, India still lacks a full regulatory framework that gives investors clear recourse. The 2022 Budget’s 30% tax on gains and 1% TDS initially cut platform turnover by about 70%, but activity has migrated to crypto derivatives — futures and options — which traders treat as business income to sidestep the punitive tax and TDS.

There is a high risk of misuse of these platforms to buy cryptos for nefarious cross-border payments.

ED probes and November 2026 searches at CoinDCX and Delta Exchange for FEMA issues underline money-laundering risks. Trading has dispersed to tier-2/3 hubs such as North 24 Parganas (≈33,000 users, ₹2,000 crore in FY26). Platforms must register with the FIU under PMLA; 53 non-compliant sites were ordered taken down. The author urges extending the 30% tax to crypto F&O and reviving international coordination on uniform rules.

Key Terminologies

  1. 30% crypto tax / 1% TDS (30% கிரிப்டோ வரி / 1% டிடிஎஸ்)
  2. Crypto futures & options (கிரிப்டோ எதிர்காலங்கள் & விருப்பங்கள்)
  3. PMLA / FIU registration (பிஎம்எல்ஏ / எஃபஐயூ பதிவு)
  4. FEMA / money laundering risk (ஃபெமா / பணமோசடி இடர்)
