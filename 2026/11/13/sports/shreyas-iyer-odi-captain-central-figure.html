Shreyas — from losing a central contract to becoming the central figure

The 31-year-old, who has enjoyed a remarkable turnaround in the last two years, will scale another high when he leads India in the fourth one-dayer against New Zealand at Mount Maunganui; he has been an asset to the National team’s 50-over set-up for a while now.

R. Kaushik

MOUNT MAUNGANUI  |  November 13, 2026

Shreyas Iyer will scale another high when he leads India in the fourth one-dayer against New Zealand at Mount Maunganui — a remarkable turnaround after he lost his central contract in early 2024 for not playing domestic cricket while recovering from injury.

He becomes India’s 29th ODI captain, standing in after Shubman Gill’s designated rest. Long consistent at No. 4 in the 50-over game — including the 2023 World Cup and the Champions Trophy in Dubai — he has also led three IPL franchises: Delhi Capitals, Kolkata Knight Riders (third title in 2024) and Punjab Kings (final in 2025).

After a shaky start to T20I captaincy (losses to Ireland and England), he found a winning formula and led India to a second successive Asian Games gold. India is one win from consigning New Zealand to a first-ever home bilateral ODI series defeat after 11 consecutive home series wins. Bay Oval pitches for the remaining matches are expected to favour the No. 4 batter, even as the short-ball challenge remains a noted technical test for him.
