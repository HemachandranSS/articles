Is Trump’s ‘no-war, no-peace’ plan on Iran sustainable in the long term?

Stanly Johny

NEW DELHI  |  November 13, 2026

After the November 3 midterms, Donald Trump has stepped back from immediate strikes on Iran while keeping a naval blockade — a no-war, no-peace approach after earlier threats to “annihilate” Iran at the UN.

Three options remain: resume strikes (risking Iranian attacks on energy sites and cables, with oil already above $100/barrel); strike a deal on Tehran’s seven demands including lifting sanctions and U.S. withdrawal (echoing a failed Islamabad MoU of June 2026); or hold the status quo of blockade and pressure. The current path is the third.

Up to 28 U.S. warships cost over $32 million a day, yet the Strait of Hormuz stays unsafe — 36 vessel attacks in October. Iranian leaders say they will break the stalemate. Easing the blockade risks Iranian control of the Strait; keeping it risks escalation. The conflict dates to February 28, 2026.

Key Terminologies

  1. No-war, no-peace (போரும் இல்லை அமைதியும் இல்லை)
  2. Naval blockade (கடற்படை முற்றுகை)
  3. Strait of Hormuz (ஹார்முஸ் நீரிணை)
  4. Sanctions / compensation demands (தடைகள் / இழப்பீட்டு கோரிக்கைகள்)
