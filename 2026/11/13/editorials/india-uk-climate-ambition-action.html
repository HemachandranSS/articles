India and U.K. are turning climate ambition into action

Ben Mellor  |  November 13, 2026

Mumbai will host the largest-ever Earthshot Prize edition next week. Founded by Prince William, the prize awards £1 million to each of five winners. India has produced more finalists and winners — nine finalists, four winners — than any other country.

The need to accelerate climate action

India’s non-fossil sources now account for more than half of installed power capacity, five years ahead of its earlier 2030 milestone framing, as it aims for 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030. The U.K. has cut greenhouse gas emissions by more than half since 1990 while growing its economy.

Financing climate action

The U.K.-India Climate Finance Initiative was launched last year to scale climate investment. The U.K. has a £330 million capital investment portfolio in India, leveraging over £3.5 billion for green businesses; British International Investment has backed hundreds of firms and committed about $1.1 billion to climate-focused investments in India since 2022. India’s Central Electricity Regulatory Commission collaborates with the U.K.’s Ofgem on market reform and offshore wind.

The U.K.-India climate partnership shows what delivery can look like in practice. Both our countries recognise that the shift to cleaner growth is not separate from the pursuit of prosperity.

A global security issue

Climate change is framed as a national security issue. The partners co-chair UN Disaster Risk Reduction senior officials’ work and look ahead to COP31 in Antalya. From Mumbai to Antalya, the message is that climate action cannot wait — the U.K. and India are building the future they need, not only talking about it.

Ben Mellor — Acting British High Commissioner to India.
