PARLEY · POLITICS · POPULISM

Has the populist far right finally entered a period of decline globally?

Christophe Jaffrelot & Irfan Nooruddin · Moderated by Sonikka Loganathan  |  November 13, 2026

In this Parley, moderated by Sonikka Loganathan, Christophe Jaffrelot and Irfan Nooruddin debate whether the populist far right is finally in global decline.

On Trump and the strongman wave

IN: Trump was a “lucky guy” who rode backlash after the first African American presidency and the post-financial-crisis economy, capitalising on prejudice and grievance politics.

CJ: He belonged to a broader wave with Orbán, Netanyahu, Erdoğan and Modi, using social media to amplify fear and anxiety.

On demography, economics and identity

In Europe, youth turn to far-right parties amid a crisis of legitimacy. Housing costs and oligarchic capitalism leave a generation that cannot reach traditional milestones and grows impatient with “slow” democracy. Identity narratives — from Europe’s “great replacement” theory to religion and race/caste politics in India — distract from socio-economic failure.

We must be careful not to mistake the rejection of Trumpism or Bolsonaro for a broader victory for liberalism. Would we get the same repudiation of Trump in an economy that’s doing much better? — Irfan Nooruddin

On institutions and a lasting decline

Whether the far right recedes depends on robust courts and election commissions, credible alternatives beyond identity markers, and a centre-right that reasserts itself without allying with the far right. Pocketbook politics and election delegitimisation (“Stop the Steal”) still shape voter behaviour. Defeat of Trumpism or Bolsonaro is not automatically a liberal victory.
