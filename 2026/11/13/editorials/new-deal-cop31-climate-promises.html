EDITORIAL · CLIMATE · COP31

New deal

Editorial  |  November 13, 2026

Nations must deliver on past promises before committing to lofty climate goals

The 31st Conference of Parties (COP31) began this week in Antalya, Türkiye. Hosted by Türkiye and negotiations presided over by Australia, the run-up saw ministerial delegations visit Fiji and Tuvalu — a reminder that small island nations are most vulnerable to warming from rising greenhouse gas emissions. Yet the opening days have belied that urgency. Prominent world leaders, even from European countries that champion the cause most vociferously, have chosen to skip the meet.

Main concerns this year are delivering on existing commitments such as $300 billion a year by 2035 (COP29, Baku) and the COP30 call to triple adaptation finance — while deciding how funds will practically flow has been deferred to COP32. The second global stocktake begins at COP31, but the IPCC has dithered on a timeline for the next reports needed to keep warming below 2°C, and more ambitiously 1.5°C.

COP30 ended without a formal roadmap away from fossil fuels — a step back from COP28 in Dubai. A voluntary roadmap ahead of COP31 sits outside the formal negotiating text. Against war in West Asia, U.S. withdrawal from the Paris Agreement and European financial strain, funds remain scarce: Nepal’s recent Loss and Damage approval of $21 million against an ask in billions underscores the gap. COP31 president Murat Kurum’s electrification target would raise electricity’s share from 23% to 35% of energy use by 2035; India has refused such a pledge and insisted on predictable climate finance, technology transfer and nationally determined approaches. Nations must deliver on past promises rather than commit to new lofty targets.
