The challenge of holding the Quad together

Harsh V. Pant & Sayantan Haldar

Op-Ed  |  November 14, 2026

At the G20 Foreign Ministers’ Meeting in Atlanta, the U.S. announced a Quad leaders’ summit in Manila in November on the ASEAN sidelines — the first under Trump’s second term and Japanese PM Sanae Takaichi.

The big ticket question confronting the Quad, however, is how Donald Trump engages with the group and the broader Indo-Pacific geography.

Harsh V. Pant and Sayantan Haldar note that despite 2025 turbulence, operational work continued — including elevating the Indo-Pacific Maritime Domain Awareness initiative to a Maritime Surveillance Collaboration framework (New Delhi, May 2026). Partners worry about U.S. focus on Ukraine and West Asia, burden-sharing pressure, and an AUKUS review. Japan’s OCEAN framework, Australia’s defence ties, India’s multi-alignment and a JAI meeting in Cebu show hedging. Manila hosting underlines ASEAN centrality and a chance to push back on Chinese pressure in the Taiwan Strait and South/East China Seas.
