Critical ban

India needs to create an entire ecosystem for recycling of lithium-ion batteries

Editorial Desk

Editorial  |  November 14, 2026

On November 10 the DGFT banned export of lithium-ion battery black mass by moving it from free to prohibited, aiming to feed domestic refiners and attract investment.

Unlike established lead-acid recycling, lithium-ion recycling has only a few dozen licensed operators. A ban without a full ecosystem risks landfill dumping. Downstream chemical processing needs capital and policy support so output can feed indigenous cell manufacture. Under the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022, cheap EPR certificates undervalue true recovery costs; a floor price plus digital tracking would help. Rare-earth magnets in phones and hard drives show similar policy gaps — e-waste rules still do not require rare-earth separation or a separate EPR category.

Key Terminologies

  1. Black mass export ban (பிளாக் மாஸ் ஏற்றுமதி தடை)
  2. Lithium-ion recycling ecosystem (லித்தியம்-அயன் மறுசுழற்சி)
  3. EPR certificates / floor price (இபிஆர் சான்றிதழ்கள் / குறைந்தபட்ச விலை)
  4. Rare earth / e-waste rules (அரிய மண் / மின்னணுக் கழிவு விதிகள்)
