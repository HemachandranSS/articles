To strengthen the Supreme Court, divide it

Pradeep S. Mehta & Vijay L. Kelkar

Op-Ed  |  November 14, 2026

Pradeep S. Mehta and Vijay L. Kelkar argue that India’s Supreme Court should be split: a lean Constitutional Court in Delhi (~11 judges) and four or five regional Courts of Appeal for ordinary litigation.

Adding judges without sorting the work risks enlarging the same overburdened court. The task is not to move the same work faster, but to sort it.

Backlog hit 92,318 cases by 2025; Constitution Benches that once decided ~134 cases a year in the 1960s now decide fewer than 10, while Special Leave Petitions under Article 136 make up ~84.6% of the docket. The 229th Law Commission (2009) and a 2023 Parliamentary panel backed regional benches. Comparisons include the U.S. Courts of Appeals, South Africa’s dual apex courts, Germany’s Federal Constitutional Court, and Brazil’s Superior Tribunal of Justice. Raising strength to 34 then 38 judges has not fixed the backlog because the work was never sorted.
