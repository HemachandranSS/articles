When boys’ voices break

Child sexual abuse of boys often goes unreported as fear, shame, and social stigma keep children and their families silent. Abhinay Deshpande reports from Telangana on how families struggle to help their children recover and seek justice, which is often delayed

Abhinay Deshpande

Ground Zero  |  November 14, 2026

Abhinay Deshpande’s Ground Zero report from Telangana examines how sexual abuse of boys stays hidden behind shame, fear of ridicule and myths about masculinity. Telangana Police Women Safety Wing data recorded 127 boy victims under the POCSO Act from Jan 2024 to Sept 2026 — rising from 4 cases in 2024 to 61 in 2025 and 62 in the first nine months of 2026.

Sexual abuse of boys may remain undisclosed because of shame, fear of ridicule, concerns about masculinity, family reputation and reluctance to acknowledge that boys can also be victims of sexual violence. — Charu Sinha, DGP (WSW)

A 2017 Indian Journal of Psychiatry study found accused persons known to the child in 61.8% of cases. Hyderabad alone has 1,769 POCSO cases pending before two designated courts. The piece stresses awareness (“good touch / bad touch”), counselling through Bharosa centres, and more courts — without interviewing child survivors directly to avoid re-traumatisation.
