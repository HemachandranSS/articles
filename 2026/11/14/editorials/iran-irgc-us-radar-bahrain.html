INTERNATIONAL RELATIONS · IRAN · U.S.

Iran’s IRGC claims it targeted a U.S. radar in Bahrain in retaliatory strike

U.S. Central Command denies the radar was destroyed, and says it intercepted two one-way drones; while Tehran says the Strait of Hormuz is closed, U.S. claims it has opened a southern route for commercial vessels to exit and enter the waters

Stanly Johny

NEW DELHI  |  November 14, 2026

The IRGC claimed it “destroyed” a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain in retaliation for a U.S. strike on a telecom tower in Sirik, southern Iran. CENTCOM denied the radar was destroyed and said it intercepted two one-way attack drones.

Tehran says the Strait of Hormuz is closed to end “air mischief and oil smuggling”; Washington says it opened a southern route for commercial ships past an IRGC blockade of Iranian ports. October saw nearly three dozen tankers hit; UKMTO reported at least six vessel attacks in the first week of November. Maj. Gen. Ali Abdollahi warned of “devastating strikes” for any U.S. or Israeli miscalculation, with the IRGC air force on full alert for “borderless confrontation.”
