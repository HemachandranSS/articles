Missing the point

FPI inflows not significantly correlated to LTCG

Editorial Desk

The Hindu Business Line  |  Monday, November 2, 2026

As the stock market struggles to recover from a two-year slump, there is rising clamour to remove long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax on equities to woo back foreign portfolio investors (FPIs). Participants argue India is among the few markets taxing foreign equity flows amid pervasive global pessimism on Indian valuations. Left unaddressed, outflows could deepen rupee weakness and erode domestic confidence.

Yet historical patterns show no robust correlation between LTCG and FPI behaviour. FPIs sold after LTCG was introduced in 2018 (FY19–FY20) but bought a record $37 billion in FY21 without any tax rollback. Inflows of $25 billion in FY24 were followed by net sales of about $15 billion in FY25, $20 billion in FY26 and $17 billion in FY27 — a cycle that tracks profit-booking after a post-Covid rebound in which Nifty 50 earnings grew 32% and annual returns near 30% looked unsustainable.

In early 2026, sentiment was hit by the Iran war, energy-import worries and trade-balance stress. FPIs also chased AI-heavy markets such as the U.S., Japan, Taiwan and South Korea, where India lacked comparable listed exposure. Whether LTCG removal is moot if fundamentals do not improve.

FPIs are more likely to return when three conditions hold: sustained real GDP growth near 7.5% and earnings momentum; policy focus on the current account deficit and inflation to stabilise the rupee; and domestic innovation that lets Indian firms capture productivity gains from AI rather than merely fund capex splurges abroad. Tax tweaks may help at the margin; they are not a substitute for macro credibility and corporate valuation discipline.
