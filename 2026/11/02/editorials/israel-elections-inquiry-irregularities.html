Israel elections chief says inquiry into irregularities won’t undermine result

Agence France-Presse

JERUSALEM  |  November 2, 2026

Central Elections Committee head Noam Sohlberg said Sunday that a probe into alleged foreign funding in Israel’s recent vote would not overturn the outcome, stressing that so far “suspicions remain suspicions.”

The Shin Bet opened the inquiry at his request to examine whether the Arab Israeli party Ra’am received money from Qatar — denied by both Doha and the party. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Likud have accused Arab groupings of “stealing” the vote in which they made unprecedented gains and could be pivotal to an Opposition-led government.

Justice Sohlberg, flanked by the Shin Bet chief and the attorney general, said elections to the 26th Knesset were conducted properly. Even if irregularities are confirmed, “this would not call into question the integrity of the electoral process or the overall results,” he said — seeking to allay concerns. The committee called suggestions of widespread fraud groundless. Netanyahu’s claims of malfeasance began on election day; Ra’am chief Mansour Abbas welcomed investigation while denying any Qatari link.
