When institutional safeguards fail

Safira Hussain & Sanjay Kumar

Op-Ed  |  November 16, 2026

After a student death at IIT Bombay, reducing the tragedy to “personal circumstances” obscures how unsupportive, discriminatory campuses shape mental distress. The Supreme Court–appointed National Task Force (2025) documented subtle caste bias in oral assessments, humiliation of reserved-category students, and scarce safe spaces to speak of discrimination.

The National Task Force’s interim report describes the Equal Opportunity Cell as one of the most neglected bodies on many campuses.

Among 1,456 surveyed institutions, 87% had an Equal Opportunity Cell, yet many barely function. UGC’s 2012 equity regulations and stalled 2026 time-bound complaint rules leave gaps; a CPRHE study across six States shows everyday exclusion (SC/ST-only remedial labels, public ‘reserved’ tagging). On mental health, 65% of 2,119 institutions lacked service access and 73% had no full-time professionals — paper cells without power.
