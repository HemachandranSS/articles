India’s green law needs baselines, not snapshots

Prasanth Raju

Op-Ed  |  November 16, 2026

On August 22 the Supreme Court set aside the National Green Tribunal’s December 2017 finding that Art of Living’s World Culture Festival organisers owed for Yamuna floodplain damage, ordering a refund of the ₹5 crore environmental deposit. The Court rightly insists that polluter pays needs a polluter — but its route leaves a gap Section 17(2) of the NGT Act had already closed.

The Yamuna floodplain verdict rightly insists that the ‘polluter pays’ principle requires a polluter, but the route it takes leaves a gap that the National Green Tribunal Act had already closed.

The Tribunal’s March 2016 interim — event allowed if organisers deposited ₹5 crore — fixed culpability without reasons after one rushed site visit; earlier records already showed a ruined floodplain. When later harm could not be separated from prior ruin, Section 17(2) allows equitable apportionment — yet the judgment quotes the section and then ignores it.

Without a recorded starting condition, every new occupant can hide fresh injury in old ruin. The fix is cheap: a signed baseline survey — levels, soil density, absorption, vegetation map, dated photos — on the day floodplain possession changes hands. Where baselines truly cannot be recovered, approximation still beats acquittal, as the ICJ showed on the Costa Rica–Nicaragua wetland claim.

Key Terminologies

  1. Polluter pays principle (மாசுபடுத்துபவர் செலுத்துக கொள்கை)
  2. NGT Act Sections 15 & 17(2) (பசுமை தீர்ப்பாயச் சட்டம் பிரிவுகள் 15 மற்றும் 17(2))
  3. Environmental baseline survey (சுற்றுச்சூழல் அடிப்படை ஆய்வு)
  4. Floodplain restoration vs upgrade (வெள்ளச் சமவெளி மீட்பு எதிர் மேம்பாடு)
