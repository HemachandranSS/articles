Looking at India’s first Prime Minister through an intelligence prism

Critics of Jawaharlal Nehru have spared no effort to portray him as being rosy-eyed and naive about China; yet, memoirs of Bhola Nath Mullik, the intelligence chief who served under Nehru for almost the entire duration of his Prime Ministership, make it clear this was far from the truth

Ramanathan Kumar

Op-Ed  |  November 16, 2026

Fresh angles on Jawaharlal Nehru are rare; Ramanathan Kumar reads him through the memoirs of B.N. Mullik, Intelligence Bureau chief for almost all of Nehru’s premiership, against the charge that Nehru was rosy-eyed about China.

Mullik joined the IB in 1948 and became DIB two years later; his three-volume trilogy covers Kashmir, the 1962 war and wider national security, portraying Nehru as a statesman with a long view. Far from naivety, Mullik shows Nehru alert to a militaristic China from about 1952 and backing a forward policy of intelligence and police posts in contested areas — seeking peace for development while preparing for conflict.

When war came in 1962, limited U.S. help followed Nehru’s appeal to JFK. Citing Bruce Riedel, Kumar closes with Mullik’s admiration for Nehru’s patriotism and vision even where they differed. (The writer is former Special Secretary, R&AW; views personal.)
