AI and the consciousness illusion

Jacob Koshy

Op-Ed  |  November 16, 2026

Fluent chatbots such as ChatGPT, Claude and DeepSeek recall the 18th-century Mechanical Turk: impressive performance that still packages human intelligence — here, vast corpora of human text — inside the machine.

The great advance of the past century and a half is the realisation that consciousness, language and intelligence need not imply each other.

Complex cerebellar calculations run without awareness; in biology, mind and wetware are not separable the way software is from silicon. Citing Ned Block and Giulio Tononi in Nature, the essay doubts conscious silicon AI. Fears of runaway AGI matter less than how firms and states may wield today’s token-shuffling tools for coercion — regulation talk from Sam Altman and Dario Amodei included — much as nuclear risk was about human use, not spontaneous machine will.
