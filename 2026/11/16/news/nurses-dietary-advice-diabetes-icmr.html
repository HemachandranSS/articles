Nurses’ dietary advice helps improve blood sugar control, finds study by Corporation, ICMR-NIE

Serena Josephine M.

CHENNAI  |  November 16, 2026

A year-long cluster randomised trial by ICMR-NIE and the Greater Chennai Corporation found that brief dietary advice from staff nurses significantly improved blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes.

In 16 urban primary health centres covering 333 adults (mostly women, average age 56), nurses gave advice every three months using the plate method — half vegetables, a quarter rice, a quarter dal, egg or fish — plus cuts in biscuits, sweets and sugary drinks. Average HbA1c fell from 9.8% to 8.5% with advice versus 9.1% under usual care, with benefits clear between months 9 and 12 and no diet-related hypoglycaemia or hospital stays.

Barriers included free rice but paid dal/eggs at ration shops, and women unable to cook separate meals. GCC is rolling the nurse-led programme out across all 140 UPHCs after October 29 training circulars for medical officers and NCD nurses.
