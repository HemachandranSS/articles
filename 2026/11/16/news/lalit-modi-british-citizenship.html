Lalit Modi gets British citizenship amid ongoing legal cases in India

The loss of Indian citizenship complicates process of extraditing IPL founder, who faces several cases and investigations in India; in 2017, the ED requested the U.K. authorities to transfer him to India to face justice in relation to non-bailable warrant

Sriram Lakshman

LONDON  |  November 16, 2026

IPL founder Lalit Modi announced he had become a British citizen, a step that ends his Indian citizenship and, legal experts say, complicates India’s efforts to extradite him to face pending cases.

He said naturalisation occurred on September 20 and formally announced it at the Oxford Union on November 14, noting the date coincided with King Charles III’s birthday. He left India in 2010 amid allegations of financial irregularities, money laundering and FEMA violations; a PMLA case and a 2015 non-bailable warrant followed. In 2017 the ED sought his transfer from the U.K.

He had earlier taken Vanuatu citizenship, leading to cancellation of his Indian passport. PM Modi had asked U.K. PM Keir Starmer in July 2025 for the return of economic offenders. Lalit Modi said he is not a “fugitive” as he has not been convicted in India.
