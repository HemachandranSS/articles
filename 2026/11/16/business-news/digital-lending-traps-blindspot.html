Need a quick loan? Beware of digital lending traps

From hidden charges and misuse of personal data to coercive recovery tactics, digital borrowing demands more than a cursory glance as borrowers need to know what the loan really costs

Sincy Wilson, Vaishali R Venkat

CHENNAI  |  November 16, 2026

Digital lending apps promise instant cash, but borrowers risk hidden charges, misuse of personal data and coercive recovery — including morphed photos and threats to contact lists — unless they read what the loan really costs.

The RBI requires lending only through regulated entities such as banks and NBFCs; lending service providers may act as agents, but the regulated entity stays responsible and must do enhanced due diligence under the Credit Facilities Directions, 2025.

Before execution, lenders must issue a Key Facts Statement disclosing APR, fees and repayment schedule. Funds must flow lender-to-borrower and repayments back to the lender — not via the app operator. Borrowers get at least a one-day cooling-off exit by repaying principal and proportionate APR without penalty. Apps may not access contacts, call logs, files or photos. Unresolved complaints after 30 days can go to the RBI Complaint Management System (cms.rbi.org.in).

Key Terminologies

  1. Regulated Entity (RE) / Lending Service Provider (LSP) (ஒழுங்குபடுத்தப்பட்ட நிறுவனம் / கடன் சேவை வழங்குநர்)
  2. Key Facts Statement (KFS) / APR (முக்கிய உண்மைகள் அறிக்கை / ஆண்டு வட்டி வீதம்)
  3. Cooling-off period (குளிர்ச்சிக் காலம்)
  4. RBI Complaint Management System (ரிசர்வ் வங்கி புகார் மேலாண்மை அமைப்பு)
