Of memoirs, autobiographies and ‘memoitographies’

Good autobiographies, biographies and memoirs are hard to come by

TCA Srinivasa Raghavan

The Hindu Business Line  |  November 16, 2026

TCA Srinivasa Raghavan separates the autobiography (whole life), the selective memoir, and his coined “memoitography” for books that blur the two — citing Sonia Gandhi’s recent volume — and notes how many life stories dwell on trivia while staying silent on what mattered.

Doctors, lawyers, judges, bureaucrats, sportspersons and even journalists have written theirs. Doctors’ autobiographies rank very high because, amongst other things, they get to see the high and mighty at their most vulnerable.

Contemporaneous diaries — BN Tandon, Wavell, Lord Moran, Pyare Lal, Kanji Dwarkadas — often cut deeper than polished memoirs. Doctors’ tales (KP Mathur, P Venugopal, Sneh Bhargava, Li Zhisui) and judges’ books (Hidayatullah, Chagla, HR Khanna, PN Bhagwati, Leila Seth, Fali Nariman) show power up close; ML Fotedar’s account closes with a glance at Congress’s leadership transition.

Key Terminologies

  1. Autobiography / memoir / memoitography (சுயசரிதை / நினைவுக்குறிப்பு / மெமாயிட்டோகிராபி)
  2. Contemporaneous diaries (அதே கால நாட்குறிப்புகள்)
  3. Doctors’ political revelations (மருத்துவர் அரசியல் வெளிப்பாடுகள்)
  4. Judicial memoirs (நீதித்துறை நினைவுக்குறிப்புகள்)
