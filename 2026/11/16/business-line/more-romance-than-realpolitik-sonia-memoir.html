More romance than realpolitik

Sonia Gandhi’s memoir is an intimate portrait of the family, not a political expose

Poornima Joshi

The Hindu Business Line  |  November 16, 2026

Poornima Joshi reviews Sonia Gandhi’s 500-plus-page Belonging: A Journey of Love (HarperCollins, ₹1,299) as an intimate family portrait and love story — not a political tell-all. The first ~200 pages linger on Italy, the wedding and arriving in India; the Emergency gets about five pages, Rajiv’s years ~94, and the last dozen BJP/Modi years only ~35.

Memorable beats include meeting Rajiv in London (un colpo di fulmine), Indira’s French welcome, loyalty that softens Emergency critiques, friction with “Chhoti Bahu” Maneka, and personal rather than investigative accounts of the assassinations. Fact slips noted include wrong presidents on a Mandela inauguration trip and a Deng visit, Ahmed Patel’s death dated 2022, and the RSS founding as 1921 instead of 1925.
