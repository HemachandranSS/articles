An extraordinary failure, and it starts at the top

Microsoft says this week’s outage wasn’t a hack and wasn’t AI. Somehow that makes it worse.

Pravin Kaushal

The Hindu Business Line  |  November 16, 2026

A Microsoft outage hit Outlook, Teams, OneDrive and Copilot across about 74 countries for nearly 18 hours. The company blamed a network capacity upgrade and a later network change — not a hack, not AI — which, Pravin Kaushal argues, makes the lapse worse: systems are breaking from the inside because management has trimmed the cushion for small mistakes.

Recovery was slow and piecemeal; a latent flaw in old code may have been tripped by the change. While AI writes much of Microsoft’s code and the firm ploughs about $175 billion into AI infra, it has cut and buyout-targeted tens of thousands of staff. Leadership that prizes shipping the next AI feature over quiet reliability leaves banks, airlines and ministries over-exposed to one vendor — something the RBI already flags when it asks banks for contingency plans.
