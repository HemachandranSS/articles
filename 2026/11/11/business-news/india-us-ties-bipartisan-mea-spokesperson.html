India-US ties enjoy bipartisan support, says MEA spokesperson

STRATEGIC PARTNERSHIP. New Delhi vows to strengthen bilateral ties despite Trump’s election setback

Our Bureau

NEW DELHI  |  November 11, 2026

The outcome of the U.S. mid-term elections is an internal matter of the country, and India-U.S. relations “enjoy strong bipartisan support” in the U.S., the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) said on Tuesday, indicating that the electoral setback suffered by President Donald Trump’s Republican Party is unlikely to affect bilateral ties.

MEA Spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said at the Ministry’s bi-weekly media briefing: “I have no specific comment on them beyond underlining that the India-United States relationship enjoys strong bipartisan support in the U.S. We continue to work to further strengthen our comprehensive global strategic partnership with the U.S.”

Trump’s setback

The Democrats made significant gains in the November 3 mid-term elections, taking control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate, ending the Republicans’ hold over Congress — a setback for Trump facing greater Congressional scrutiny during the remaining two years of his presidency.

India’s position

On the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, India reiterated its call for free and safe navigation through the strategically important waterway, amid concerns over attacks on commercial shipping and seafarers. Jaiswal said India is in touch with all concerned parties to avoid attacks on civilian shipping, including vessels under the Indian flag.

On Monday, Prime Minister Narendra Modi spoke with President Zelenskyy, following which the External Affairs Minister spoke with his Russian counterpart. “On the issue of food corridors, we have exchanged ideas. India remains in touch with both sides, as well as other interested parties, to contribute towards the return of peace to the region,” Jaiswal said.
