Sodium-ion batteries emerge as cheaper alternative to lithium-ion battery

Rishi Ranjan Kala

NEW DELHI  |  November 11, 2026

Sodium-ion batteries (SIB) are fast gaining traction globally, including India, with factors such as supply chain security, cycle life and wide thermal threshold pushing towards rapid commercialisation.

A recent report by Wood Mackenzie indicates that SIBs are entering commercial production with more than 70 gigawatt-hour (GWh) of supply agreements, signalling confidence among downstream clients. The report finds that SIB is establishing itself as the most cost-effective emerging alternative to lithium-ion battery storage.

While cell-level parity with lithium iron phosphate (LFP) amongst market leaders is expected by the end of 2026, module and system-level cost parity will be attained post-2030.

SIB command

Wood Mackenzie notes SIB commands higher upfront capital at $229 per kWh versus $200 for LFP but delivers 21 per cent lower total cost of ownership over a 20-year project life, stemming from eliminating augmentation events and lowering operating expenditure.

Consumption tax

China’s reimposition of a consumption tax on lithium-ion in July 2026 explicitly exempts sodium-ion through end-2028, while LFP faces a 2 per cent levy from September 2026, rising to 4 per cent from September 2027. In the U.S., Section 301 tariffs of approximately 58 per cent on Chinese energy storage cells incentivise domestic SIB production, complemented by IRA 45X manufacturing credits. India is building local capacity through a $1.25 billion incentive scheme and duty exemptions on manufacturing equipment.

For projects being deployed today, LFP remains the bankable choice for 2- to 4-hour-duration assets, while the 2030-35 window is where economics decisively shift. BYD has 30 GWh of planned manufacturing capacity in China, while CATL is targeting at least 1 GWh of SIB shipments by the end of 2026.
