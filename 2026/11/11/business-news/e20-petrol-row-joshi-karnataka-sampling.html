E20 petrol row: MNRE Minister Joshi faults Karnataka’s sampling process

Our Bureau

NEW DELHI  |  November 11, 2026

Minister for New and Renewable Energy Pralhad Joshi has disputed the Karnataka government’s findings on the percentage of ethanol in petrol, claiming that the requisite procedures for sampling and testing were not followed.

“The Karnataka certificates state only ‘Ethanol % by Gas Chromatography’. They do not mention the test method, calibration, dilution or reference sample,” Joshi, who is also the Minister for Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, said on X late on Monday.

Karnataka’s claims

Karnataka’s Department of Food, Civil Supplies, Consumer Affairs and Legal Metrology had claimed that petrol sold at certain retail outlets in the State contained 26-31 per cent ethanol.

Samples showing 26-31 per cent ethanol content in Karnataka recorded densities of 0.742-0.749 g/cc. But samples tested by oil marketing companies, which confirmed compliant E20 petrol, recorded higher densities of 0.752-0.760 g/cc, Joshi added.

He emphasised that none of the labs that tested these samples were part of the approved Schedule 3 labs notified in the Gazette. The deficiencies in Karnataka’s sampling procedure, retention samples and laboratory certification raise serious questions about the credibility of the measurement, he added.

Joshi noted that in 2025-26 Karnataka distilleries were allocated 165.89 crore litres of ethanol, and of the 30 lakh tonnes of sugar diverted for ethanol across India, Karnataka alone accounted for 11.92 lakh tonnes as of August 31, 2026 — nearly 40 per cent of the national diversion.

Cost-saving programme

The ethanol programme has helped India save over ₹1.97 lakh crore in foreign exchange, substitute approximately 316 lakh tonnes of crude oil imports and reduce CO2 emissions by 952 lakh tonnes.

Last week, responding to claims by Karnataka, oil marketing companies said in a joint statement that the certificates do not provide the complete technical details necessary to independently verify the reported ethanol content, and that the absence of counterpart samples denied dealers an opportunity to seek verification or retesting.
