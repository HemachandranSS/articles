MFs’ Nifty 50 allocation falls to 21-year low in Q2

BROADER BETS. Exposure slips below 50% as funds widen portfolio beyond top stocks

Akshata Gorde

MUMBAI  |  November 11, 2026

Domestic mutual funds’ allocation to Nifty 50 stocks fell below 50 per cent for the first time in 21 years in the September quarter, even as they increased exposure to other relatively large- and mid-cap companies, amid stronger returns from the wider market.

The 50 benchmark stocks accounted for 49.4 per cent of mutual funds’ holdings in NSE-listed equities, down from 51.4 per cent in June and a peak of 63 per cent in December 2019, according to the National Stock Exchange’s India Ownership Tracker. The allocation had recovered during 2025 as large-cap stocks held up relatively better, before falling again this year.

Higher exposure

Yet, mutual funds increased their exposure to the largest 10 per cent of NSE-listed companies by market capitalisation by 4.2 percentage points to 87.6 per cent. Within this group, their allocation to companies outside the Nifty 50 rose by 6.1 percentage points, largely reflecting higher exposure to mid-cap companies, the NSE said.

Their combined allocation to the next two market-cap groups, comprising mainly small- and micro-cap stocks, declined by 3.1 percentage points to 11.7 per cent.

Atul Singhal, Founder and CEO of Scripbox, said foreign investors pulled about $65 billion out over two years, mostly from large-caps, while $3 billion a month of SIP money is going into funds that can invest beyond the index.

The Nifty 50 delivered annualised returns of 4.8 per cent over the three years ended September compared with 13 per cent for the Nifty Midcap 150 and 13.3 per cent for the Nifty Smallcap 250. ICRA Analytics’ September screener showed average annualised three-year returns of 14.22 per cent for mid-cap funds and 14.49 per cent for small-cap schemes, against 8.05 per cent for large-cap funds.

High-growth hunt

According to the latest data of the Association of Mutual Funds in India, small-cap funds attracted ₹9,039 crore and mid-cap schemes ₹7,835 crore in September, together accounting for about 44 per cent of the ₹38,307 crore net inflows into equity schemes. Large-cap funds received ₹859 crore.

Even with the falling portfolio weight, the ownership of mutual funds in Nifty 50 companies’ market capitalisation has risen to a record 14.8 per cent in September, marking the 13th consecutive quarterly increase. Overall, institutional allocation to the index fell to a record low 54.6 per cent, with foreign portfolio investors’ allocation declining to 56.2 per cent.
