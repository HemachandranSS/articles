Crude oil: A breather for India

The Democratic landslide in US may push global oil prices down. India must tap this opportunity

Richa Mishra

The Hindu Business Line  |  November 11, 2026

When the American voters handed Democrats control of Congress in the recent mid-term elections, the ramifications were felt far beyond Washington. Within days, global crude prices retreated sharply below the $100 mark, with Brent dipping toward $93 and West Texas Intermediate (WTI) hovering near $85 a barrel. Markets immediately priced in a more constrained White House, one facing stiff legislative resistance to military escalation in the Middle East and unilateral tariff wars.

For New Delhi, this respite offers a breathing space, though it must maintain discipline. It also leads to certain questions: if the threat of strict secondary U.S. sanctions softens due to a more cautious, inflation-sensitive U.S. Congress, should India deepen its long-term contracts for Russian crude, or maintain the current flexible, month-by-month spot buying approach? How will OPEC+ respond to the new situation and falling oil prices?

Oil price impact

India’s economy has so far weathered the storm of conflict-driven oil surges. With a projected global oil surplus and congressional pressure on Trump, downward pressure on prices should hold over the medium term. But policymakers in India may resist the populist temptation to slash domestic fuel excise duties.

India may resist the temptation to slash fuel excise duties. Holding retail petrol and diesel prices steady will allow PSU retailers to repair their balance sheets.

Though Trump has for now paused fresh strikes on Iran, a durable peace still remains elusive. India must maintain naval escorts in the Gulf of Oman and lean heavily on overland pipelines through Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Policy gridlock in a divided Washington increases the risk of erratic executive pivots. India should seize this price dip to top off its Strategic Petroleum Reserves and fast-track the four-million-tonne Chandikhol facility, insulating the economy against future shocks.

Russian oil buys

On Russian crude, India should maintain its strategic agility and stick to its nimble, month-by-month spot-buying playbook rather than signing rigid long-term contracts. Lauri Myllyvirta, lead analyst at the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air, believes that Democratic control of the House will be bearish for oil prices, though upside risks remain from Hormuz or Bab el-Mandeb disruptions and renewed Chinese buying.

OPEC+ response

Traders and analysts hold that OPEC+ is likely to pause planned output hikes to defend an $85–$95 price floor, though sluggish global demand will keep the cartel from imposing draconian cuts.

This shifting political landscape also strengthens India’s hand at the trade negotiating table ahead of the G20 summit in Miami. Indian negotiators must stand firm, keeping energy targets as non-binding commercial aspirations while using selective agricultural market access as leverage to demand the rollback of the 18 per cent U.S. reciprocal tariff on Indian textiles and machinery. A newly emboldened green bloc on Capitol Hill opens a fresh avenue for clean-tech collaboration through India’s PLI scheme for advanced battery storage paired with U.S. Development Finance Corporation capital.

The U.S. mid-terms have handed India a rare geopolitical pause. By combining fiscal restraint at home with shrewd diplomacy abroad, New Delhi can turn a temporary market dip into lasting strategic resilience.
