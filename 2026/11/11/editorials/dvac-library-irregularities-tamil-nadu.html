TAMIL NADU · EDUCATION · LIBRARIES

DVAC launches investigation into allegations of financial irregularities at libraries in T.N.

DVAC officers scrutinised records at Anna Centenary Library and Periyar Arivulagam. School Education Minister Rajmohan says discussions are on to establish a committee to purchase books for libraries in a transparent manner and book procurement for new Tiruchi library has been cancelled

The Hindu Bureau

CHENNAI/COIMBATORE/TIRUCHI  |  November 11, 2026

The Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC) has launched a detailed inquiry into allegations of serious financial irregularities at Anna Centenary Library in Chennai and Periyar Arivulagam library in Coimbatore.

DVAC officers perused records in both the libraries on Monday and Tuesday, which followed a statement made by School Education Minister Rajmohan alleging that irregularities took place during the erstwhile DMK regime in the purchase of books for the newly-launched Periyar Arivulagam library in Coimbatore. Amid allegations that books with AI-generated content and factual mistakes were purchased at inflated rates, the officers made inquiries with the library staff and took copies of certain records from both libraries for investigation, sources in the agency said.

The irregularities were flagged by noted Tamil writer Perumal Murugan, who visited the library in Coimbatore recently and pointed to issues relating to price, quality, and authenticity of the content in some of the books. The agency is looking into allegations of names of fictitious authors, fabrication of ISBNs, depiction of factual errors, and inflated book prices.

DMK moves HC

DMK Legal Wing joint secretary I. Paranthamen has filed a writ petition in the Madras High Court seeking a direction to the DVAC to register an FIR on the basis of a complaint lodged by him regarding alleged irregularities in procurement of books for Periyar Arivulagam. He said Agal Vilakku Publications and Phoenix Publications appeared to have supplied books generated using AI tools rather than having been written by genuine authors.

Procurement cancelled

The procurement order for books for the new Kamarajar Grand Library and Intellectual Centre (KGLIC) in Tiruchi has been cancelled. Books were planned to be procured at an estimated cost of about ₹30 crore for the Kamarajar Library, but no expenditure has been incurred under this head so far. Publishers have been asked to take back their book submissions; 136 publishers had submitted publications and 217 unopened bundles are stored at the District Central Library in Tiruchi.

Mr. Rajmohan said on Tuesday that discussions were under way to establish a committee to purchase books for libraries in a transparent manner. New book purchase orders will be given by December for all the libraries. He said authorities would identify all the fake books and “seal” them, adding that no books have been purchased since the government came to power.
