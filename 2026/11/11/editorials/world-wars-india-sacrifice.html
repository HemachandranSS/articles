World Wars and India’s sacrifice

Priyanjali Malik

Writes on politics and international relations. In the interest of full disclosure, the three Bhagat brothers mentioned were her father’s cousins.

Op-Ed  |  November 11, 2026

On the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month, most Commonwealth and other allied nations fall silent for two minutes to honour the fallen of the two World Wars. Not so much in India: wreaths are laid in cantonments, but for most, this is just another day. India has an uneasy relationship with Remembrance Day. However, the idea that the World Wars were something that happened to someone else, somewhere else, could not be further from the truth.

Indian ambivalence over the two World Wars belies its enormous contribution to fighting fascism. Undivided (British) India sent 1.5 million men to fight in World War I, 74,000 of whom never returned home. For WWII, India produced the largest volunteer army in history with 2.5 million soldiers, with 87,000–89,000 making the ultimate sacrifice. The princely states also sent significant troops. Whereas the Indians who fought in the Great War were mostly sent to distant lands, in WWII, about 7,00,000 of them were defending Indian territory. The war came home to India when Japan invaded the country in March 1944.

The argument that the Indian soldiers who fought under British command were defending freedom for the British while their country’s aspirations for the same freedoms remained suppressed under colonial rule is legitimate. Yet independent India’s ambivalence over WWII goes deeper. The fact that the adversary in this case included an Asian power claiming Asian solidarity has sometimes obscured the fact that imperial Japan was pushing into India in a brutal campaign of expansion.

And then there is the complicated history of the Indian National Army (INA), led by Subhas Chandra Bose. The INA, which was initially formed under Captain Mohan Singh in 1942, comprised mainly 40,000 Indian Prisoners of War (PoWs) who were captured and tortured by Japan when Singapore fell in 1942. It was re-formed by Bose when he joined the Japanese in Singapore in July 1943. Bose expanded the INA, or the Azad Hind Fauj, setting up a government in exile. However, nothing about the provisional government of free India could disguise the fact that several of the men who joined the INA did so under duress. Bose teaming with the Japanese pitted aspirations for freedom against the lived reality of occupation.

Fallen heroes

On April 6, 1942, the war came to India with Japanese aircraft bombing ships sheltering in the Vizag harbour and the port. In December of that year, Kolkata was bombed over four days, with the Japanese targeting the city’s infrastructure. The following December, Japanese aircraft bombed Kolkata’s Kidderpore dock, killing hundreds of people and causing significant damage. From January 1942, the Japanese Army swept through Southeast Asia, overrunning Myanmar and occupying Burma. The fighting in the Burma campaign, which involved over 7,00,000 Indians, was brutal, not just because of Japanese tactics, but also because of the forbidding jungle terrain. In March 1944, the Japanese Army attempted a land invasion of India from Myanmar. The Japanese calculated that once they entered, the locals could be encouraged to revolt against the British. However, Indians decided against trading British rule for the Japanese.

India was defended by Indians in some of the fiercest fighting of the war. Indians (from undivided India) made up 87% of the multinational fighting force troops in the Burma campaign. A disciplined fighting force was forged in the heat of this combat; and this force has defended India after 1947.

The first Indian to be awarded a Victoria Cross (VC) in WWII was Second-Lt (later Lt-Gen) Premindra Singh Bhagat for ‘most conspicuous gallantry on active service’ clearing mines in East Africa in early 1942. In late 1943, his eldest brother, Nripendra Bhagat, was taken PoW by the Japanese while fighting in Burma. While in prison, he was made to broadcast a propaganda piece, almost certainly under torture. He was handed over to the INA, rising quite high in the ‘provisional government’. He subsequently disagreed with Bose and was imprisoned by him in Burma. He escaped and was captured by Indian soldiers, who handed him over to the middle brother, Brijindra, who was with the 5th Indian Division in Burma. All three were fierce nationalists and one story cannot be told without mentioning the others.

Soon after Prem Bhagat was awarded the VC, he went to see Mahatma Gandhi. How could he work for his country, he asked Gandhi. The answer was simple: stay in the army. India would need men like him to defend her. History is complicated. However, we owe it to ourselves to be honest with our past, just as we owe it to our armed forces to honour the sacrifices of those who went before them.

“We owe it to ourselves to be honest with our past, just as we owe it to our armed forces to honour the sacrifices of those who went before them.”
