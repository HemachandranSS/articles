U.S. court permits suits on NSA Doval, RAW ex-chief Goel

Order issued in civil plea by Khalistani separatist Pannun linked to an alleged assassination plot against him; India rejects charges as ‘frivolous’

Suhasini Haidar

NEW DELHI  |  November 11, 2026

A U.S. court on Monday allowed Khalistani separatist and lawyer Gurpatwant Singh Pannun to serve his civil plea on National Security Adviser (NSA) Ajit Doval and former Research and Analysis Wing (RAW) chief Samant Goel through Indian judicial channels, after the State Department submitted that it had served the notice on officials at the Indian Embassy in Washington.

The External Affairs Ministry rejected the move, calling the lawsuit “frivolous” and saying such judicial documents must be sent to the Law and Justice Ministry in India.

“We reject the fabricated and malicious attacks on our high officials, as also we reject the frivolous lawsuits.” — Randhir Jaiswal, MEA spokesperson

Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said request for serving of judicial documents in India can only be routed through India’s designated central authority, that is the Ministry of Law and Justice. The lawsuit filed by Pannun, wanted in India on terror charges, is separate but linked to the FBI’s findings of alleged Indian links to an assassination plot against him in June 2023.

Granting permission for the summons on Monday, U.S. District Judge Katherine Polk Failla directed Pannun to prepare documents on or before November 30, including the summons addressed to Mr. Doval and Mr. Goel, details of Indian law and procedure, and the route by which the summons would be issued, as well as records of previous attempts at serving the two officials under the Hague Convention.

The certificate of submission delivered to the Indian Embassy by the State Department was signed and dated October 9, 2026. The State Department said delivery did not mean it was taking a position on the case and it was a legal obligation.
