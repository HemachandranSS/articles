John Harrison’s The End of Everything wins the 2026 Booker Prize for fiction

Associated Press

LONDON  |  November 11, 2026

British writer M. John Harrison won the 2026 Booker Prize on Tuesday for his satirical dystopian novel The End of Everything, becoming the first science-fiction work to take the award.

Harrison, 81, has been writing for six decades and said the era of “genres as fences and boxes” is over. He beat five other finalists, including previous winners Douglas Stuart and Marlon James, and Pulitzer laureate Elizabeth Strout.

Set on England’s Channel coast in the near future after a calamity, the book depicts society shattered by catastrophe and apocalypse, an influx of enigmatic alien entities, and a beachcomber living with a gun-toting aunt who finds a shape-shifting artifact.

“It’s a grim book, it’s a weird book,” Harrison said after receiving the trophy at Old Billingsgate in London. “But at the end of things there may be some kind of — I wouldn’t say hope, or closure, but there might be some kind of relief.”
