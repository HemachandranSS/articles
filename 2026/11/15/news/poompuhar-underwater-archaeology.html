Surfacing: resurrecting the lost glory of Poompuhar

Recent underwater explorations off the coast of Poompuhar seem to have yielded a wealth of artefacts indicating the presence of an advanced society that thrived during the Sangam period

N. Sai Charan

MAYILADUTHURAI  |  November 15, 2026

For nearly 2,000 years, the history of Poompuhar survived mainly through Sangam literature and shoreline finds. Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology divers have now completed three phases of underwater survey off the coast.

Teams covered about 16 km between Thirumullaivasal and Vanagiri, up to six kilometres offshore, using side-scan sonar, DGPS, ROVs and dive computers. The third zone (2–5.5 km) yielded ring wells and more than 250 ceramic pieces, including a West Asian-style torpedo jar fragment — the first of its kind in an underwater survey here.

Researchers are also re-documenting a previously identified shipwreck after finding additional lead ingots, seeking firmer archaeological proof of the submerged city’s maritime trade links.
