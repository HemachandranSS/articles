Question Corner: Why do firecracker emissions sometimes linger in the air?

Smoke in the air — inversions and shallow boundary layers trap pollutants near the ground

The Hindu

SCIENCE  |  November 15, 2026

Emissions from firecrackers linger when local atmospheric conditions keep pollutants from dispersing. During an inversion, warmer air aloft traps surface smoke.

When the lowest atmosphere — the boundary layer — becomes shallow, pollutants stay confined close to the ground instead of mixing upward.

Weak winds compound the problem, so festive emissions can hang for hours even after bursting stops.
