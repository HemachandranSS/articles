How Microsoft outage affected millions

When the problem first emerged, Microsoft engineers and automated systems shifted their workload to other machines, which then became overloaded and failed as well, thus triggering a chain reaction through its networks that affected multiple services

Sahana Venugopal, Vasudevan Mukunth

CHENNAI  |  November 15, 2026

Software giant Microsoft faced a widespread outage affecting Microsoft 365 services from the early hours of November 13, disrupting email, calendars and collaboration tools used by millions of paid subscribers.

Exchange Online and Teams were hit hardest; education tools such as Assignments and Reading Progress also failed. Microsoft expanded the delivery timeout to 48 hours to clear backlogs — not because mail was offline for two days, but so servers would keep retrying. VP Charles Lamanna described cascading failures and a thundering herd at the network perimeter.

Customers criticised opaque technical updates; speculation about AI or cyberattacks was denied. The episode renewed calls for governments and critical enterprises to diversify IT providers and avoid single points of failure.
