TAXATION · TAMIL NADU · REAL ESTATE

Reset tax on transfer of property

R. Srinivasan & S. Raja Sethu Durai

Former Professor, Dept. of Econometrics, University of Madras; and Professor of Economics, University of Hyderabad.

Op-Ed  |  November 12, 2026

Stamp duty and Registration Fees (SDR) are major components of States’ own-source tax revenue in India. In 2024-25, 11.7% of States’ own tax revenue was collected through SDR. Tamil Nadu collected 11.3% of its own tax revenue through this source. However, it has not harnessed this source to the same extent as Maharashtra and Karnataka, which collected 14.8% and 12.3% respectively. The Tamil Nadu government has recently constituted the Revenue Augmentation Committee to strengthen own-tax and non-tax revenues.

SDRs are taxes imposed on the legal transfer of ownership of immovable property. Hence, they increase the property’s total effective cost. The State government fixes a guideline value and restricts registration of legal transfers below it. In practice, the actual value of the property being transferred is far above the guideline value. This can be addressed only through a scientific process of fixing guideline values and revising them periodically. At the same time, the SDR rate should encourage parties to declare the true value of the property being transferred.

A robust guideline value determination system depends on transparent methodology, open data, annual revision, and the autonomy of the guideline value fixation committee.

Tamil Nadu has a total SDR of 9%. Compare this with the SDR of 6% in Karnataka and Maharashtra. Maharashtra also has a monetary cap of ₹30,000 on registration fees. While the number of documents registered in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Maharashtra were 33.6 lakh, 25 lakh, and 30 lakh respectively, Karnataka and Maharashtra collected roughly ₹24,000 crore and ₹50,000 crore — far higher than the ₹22,000 crore collected by Tamil Nadu in 2024-25.

Fixing the process

Lower conveyance costs could encourage the short-term secondary housing market. The government could introduce a graduated stamp-duty concession for developers who sell properties within one to two years of completion. Buyers will be incentivised to declare the actual purchase price if stamp duty is reduced by a few percentage points for each increase in the declared purchase price. There should also be a cap on registration fees, since the cost of registering documents does not increase with property value.

For guideline value fixation, spatial analytics is necessary, including inputs on recent neighbourhood sale prices, prevailing rental values, and the development of public utilities. Revisions should occur at least once a year. Fixing the normal SDR to encourage parties to reveal the actual price, and creating an autonomous process for setting the guideline value as the minimum tax base, are key features of a good taxing system for property transfers.
