DATA POINT · POLLUTION · DEEPAVALI

Across India, 70% of stations reported ‘severe’ PM2.5 readings

Pon Vasanth B.A

Data Point  |  November 12, 2026

Apart from Delhi, at least seven States reported peak PM2.5 levels during Deepavali

While Delhi and a few other major cities have been the key focus of the alarming spike in air pollution during Deepavali, analyses of data from all the 634 Continuous Ambient Air Quality Monitoring Stations (CAAQMS) in the Central Pollution Control Board’s (CPCB) network showed that nearly 70% of the stations across the country recorded peak PM2.5 readings in the ‘severe’ category of 250 micrograms per cubic metre or above.

Moreover, data showed that many stations partially or fully stopped reporting PM2.5 values during Deepavali even before they were recording values closer to 1,000 µg/m³. CPCB said particulate matter analysers were calibrated for a measurement range of 1,000 µg/m³, and automated validation may appear as a data gap during extreme episodes. Only 571 of the 634 stations were live and reporting data during Deepavali.

Widespread increase

Of 551 stations that reported PM2.5 at least once during Deepavali, 375 (nearly 70%) recorded 250 µg/m³ or more at least once. Only 96 had recorded PM2.5 in this range in the pre-Deepavali window. Among States with at least 20 stations, more than two-thirds of stations in Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat and Andhra Pradesh reported peak levels in the ‘severe’ category. Tamil Nadu stood out not for reduced pollution but for absence of data: despite 37 stations, only nine reported PM2.5 at least once during the Deepavali hourly windows.
