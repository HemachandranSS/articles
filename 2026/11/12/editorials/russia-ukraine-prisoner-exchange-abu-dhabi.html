Russia, Ukraine agree to exchange prisoners as peace talks conclude

The two sides remain far apart on central issues needed for a peace settlement; U.S. envoys to travel to Moscow to meet Putin and to Dublin to meet Zelenskyy; key hurdles for negotiators include postwar security guarantees for both countries

Associated Press

ABU DHABI  |  November 12, 2026

Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large exchange of prisoners of war, both sides said on Wednesday as two days of U.S.-mediated talks concluded, but they announced no major breakthrough on ending the conflict that began after Moscow’s full-scale invasion nearly five years ago.

Hopes had been low for any significant development in the first meeting in nine months between the delegations. Russia and Ukraine have from time to time swapped POWs but have remained far apart in their demands for accepting a peace settlement.

U.S. officials say they are determined to revive Washington’s peace efforts that so far have failed to deliver significant results. As well as postwar security guarantees for both countries, other key hurdles for negotiators are the fate of Russian-occupied Ukrainian territory and what role European countries will play in an eventual settlement.

The session was the first such meeting since February on Russia’s invasion. The talks lasted just under two hours on Wednesday, after a four-hour session the previous day.

“One of the positives is we agreed that we’re going to have the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners, which we’re going to finalise the numbers tomorrow,” said Jared Kushner, U.S. President Donald Trump’s son-in-law, after the talks in Abu Dhabi ended.

Vladimir Medinsky, the head of the Russian delegation, and Rustem Umerov, who led Kyiv’s contingent, confirmed the planned POW exchange, although Mr. Medinsky noted that the agreement was “in principle”. Mr. Kushner said he and U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff will travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet with President Vladimir Putin, then fly to Dublin, Ireland, to meet with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.
