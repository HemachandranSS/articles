Netanyahu rejects remarks blaming Israel for the flood crisis in Gaza Strip

Agence France-Presse

JERUSALEM  |  November 12, 2026

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has rejected accusations that Israel has been delaying the entry of winter supplies into the Gaza Strip as severe rains flood makeshift tents, worsening conditions in the enclave this week.

He instead blamed Hamas, saying the Palestinian militant group refuses to disarm and seizes humanitarian aid for military use. He provided no evidence for the aid seizure claim.

His remarks came after the U.S.-created Board of Peace said on Monday that Israel’s delays in allowing winter supplies into Gaza had “exacerbated the consequences of a foreseeable humanitarian challenge”.

“The situation no longer represents simply a threat posed by the approaching winter, but an immediate humanitarian emergency,” the Board of Peace said in a statement.

Heavy rain over the past two days has swept away tens of thousands of tents in the territory, where virtually the entire population has been displaced at least once, and killed at least five people, according to Hamas-led authorities in Gaza. The Board of Peace, tasked with implementation of an October 2025 U.S.-brokered ceasefire, said it had been warning Israel, which controls all entry into the territory, of the need to prepare both for the winter and seasonal flooding.

Moments before the statement came out, the Israeli military said it had allowed more than 8,200 trucks to enter the Gaza Strip with supplies including 91,000 tents. The Board of Peace acknowledged steps being taken by Israel but said that they remain “insufficient”. It also called on Hamas to abide by commitments under the ceasefire.
