‘Affordability crisis’ and US polls

Rise in living costs is cited as the main reason for the Republican defeat in the mid-terms. But there were other factors at play

C.P. Chandrasekhar & Jayati Ghosh

The Hindu Business Line  |  November 12, 2026

There is broad consensus around the view that the higher costs of living, or what is generally known as the “affordability crisis”, is the main reason for the Republican defeat in the U.S. mid-term elections. But there were other factors at play. People typically do not compare their conditions with those in other countries, but use the benchmark of their own recent past experience. Consumer prices that rise faster than money wage rates erode people’s ability to keep up earlier levels of consumption.

While average real wages peaked in 2020 and then fell quite sharply, they had been rising since then. However, there has indeed been a fall in real wages in the current year, 2026, which is evident more clearly in the monthly data.

Iran war

Opinion polls suggest that President Trump’s increasingly unfavourable ratings began with the unprovoked war on Iran. The Iranian response, which showed both resilience of the regime and the ability to block traffic in the Strait of Hormuz, affected global oil supplies. Higher grocery bills and prices at the pump were immediately felt due to massive reliance on private transport. Other costs also made matters worse: higher mortgage payments, bigger health insurance payouts, and rising urban rents.

Growing inequality

The main reason for the electorate’s dissatisfaction: very high and rising inequality. The wage share of national income in the U.S. has been declining since 1970, with a further steep decline especially since 2020.

The wage share of national income in the U.S. has been declining since 1970; wages and salaries now account for just over half of GDP, compared to nearly 60% in 1970. The top 10% of wage earners generally received around two-and-a-half times the income shares of the bottom half of the population — significantly higher than the OECD average.

The Trump regime has been more openly partisan toward digital giants and crypto fintech companies that have received subsidies and favourable regulatory treatment. Many polls found that corruption was the second most important concern among voters, followed by the Iran war. The affordability crisis is just one aspect of the generalised failure of this specific form of crony capitalism, which enriches those close to power and impoverishes the rest.
