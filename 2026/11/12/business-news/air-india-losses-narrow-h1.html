Air India’s losses narrow in H1 by ₹2,000 crore

Jagriti Chandra

NEW DELHI  |  November 12, 2026

Singapore Airlines’ share of losses from Air India (AI) declined to S$361.7 million (about ₹2,720 crore) in the first half of FY27, down from S$429.5 million (₹3,229 crore) in the corresponding period last year, marking a reduction of S$67.8 million (about ₹510 crore).

Based on Singapore Airlines’ 25.1% stake in Air India, the figures imply the Tata Group-owned airline’s total losses fell to an estimated S$1.44 billion (₹10,837 crore) from S$1.71 billion (₹12,850 crore) a year earlier — a narrowing of roughly ₹2,000 crore.

AI, along with AI Express, reported a consolidated net loss of ₹22,238 crore last financial year.

Some relief

The moderation in losses offers some relief to Air India’s financial backers, even as Singapore Airlines reported a 43.9% dip in its own first-half net profit to S$134 million (about ₹1,008 crore), amid a sharp increase in jet fuel costs. Air India, along with its subsidiary Air India Express, reported a consolidated net loss of ₹22,238 crore in FY26, more than double the ₹10,859 crore loss recorded in the previous financial year.

Singapore Airlines said it and Tata Sons recognise “that a transformation of this scale will take time,” and continue to work on structural cost efficiencies and synergies between Air India and Air India Express. Cost rationalisation emerged as a key priority under AI’s new CEO and MD, Tewolde Gebremariam, who said on November 3 that the airline was reviewing unit costs and had set up a multi-functional cost-savings team.
