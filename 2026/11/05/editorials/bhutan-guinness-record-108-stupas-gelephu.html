Bhutan sets Guinness World Record for erecting 108 stupas in nine hours

Rahul Karmakar

GELEPHU  |  November 5, 2026

Bhutan set a Guinness World Record by erecting 108 Buddhist stupas — Jangchub Chortens — in nine hours, GMC Governor Lotay Tshering said, receiving the certificate Tuesday at Gelephu.

About 40,000 trained volunteers raised the chortens from 3 a.m. to 11.55 a.m. on November 1, marking the 18th coronation anniversary of the King. Teams of 20–23 worked simultaneously along the Mau Chhu River, each structure 14 metres tall and 108 metres apart — a feat officials called unprecedented.

The King envisaged Project 108 as tradition rooted yet forward-looking, and as foundation for GMC — a spirituality-based ethical global economic hub. Each chorten cost about $200,000 with global donors. Chorten No. 76 honoured India’s steadfast commitment to the city’s realisation — soft-power diplomacy tying cultural heritage to India–Bhutan development partnership.
