Indian-American Democratic lawmakers make strong showing in U.S. midterm polls

Ami Bera, Ro Khanna, Suhas Subramanyam and Pramila Jayapal retain their seats in the House of Representatives; Pia Dandiya enters Congress for the first time; Trump-backed Republican Vivek Ramaswamy loses Ohio Governor’s race; Indian-American community is estimated to be over 5 mn.

Press Trust of India

WASHINGTON  |  November 5, 2026

Several Indian-American lawmakers posted wins in midterm polls, retaining or expanding Democratic footholds in the House of Representatives.

Incumbent Ami Bera (61) won California’s 3rd congressional district for an eighth term — the longest-serving Indian-American in Congress — and sits on the House Foreign Affairs Committee. Ro Khanna (50) held Silicon Valley’s 17th district for a fifth term. Suhas Subramanyam (40) took Virginia’s 10th with over 61% after advising Barack Obama. Pramila Jayapal secured a sixth term in Washington’s 7th.

Pia Dandiya (39) made her debut in Florida’s 22nd with 51.9%. Maryland Lieutenant Governor Aruna Miller won another term. Trump-backed Republican Vivek Ramaswamy lost the gubernatorial contest in Ohio to Democrat Amy Acton. The community, estimated above 5 million, is the second-largest Asian-origin population in the U.S. — a legislatures story tying diaspora clout to primary and general-election coalitions.
