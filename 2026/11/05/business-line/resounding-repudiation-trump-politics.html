OPINION · UNITED STATES

A resounding repudiation of Trump’s politics

The Democrats now must put up a united front to tackle the key issues of affordability and immigration

Sridhar Krishnaswami

Senior journalist; former Washington and UN correspondent  |  November 5, 2026

Donald Trump may insist that Republican midterm losses occur only when his name is not on the ballot. Yet November 3 was widely treated as a referendum on Trump personally. Voters handed Congress to Democrats — a sharp repudiation of the President and the Grand Old Party.

The House and Senate flipped from red to blue. Republicans stumbled in Texas and Ohio; Susan Collins fell in Maine; association with “Trump 2.0” became a liability. Unpopular Iran-war choices, high pump prices and grocery inflation linked to tariffs and sanctions crowded the agenda alongside immigration enforcement.

Trump hangover

The ICE crackdown’s overbearing tone enraged Hispanic communities and moderates. Affordability and immigration now dominate the Democratic mandate; internal infighting must yield to a united front if gains are to last through 2028.

Congress may revisit the 1973 War Powers Act, press accountability on corruption probes, and force debate on ending Middle East military escalation. Trump, in a post-election interview, signalled willingness to work with Democrats — though whether that survives committee gavels and primary pressures remains open.
