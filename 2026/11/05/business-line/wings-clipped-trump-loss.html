Wings clipped

Trump’s loss may be good news for the world

Editorial Desk

The Hindu Business Line  |  Thursday, November 5, 2026

The American midterm results land as a body blow to the economic and strategic warfare President Donald Trump waged at home and abroad. Having turned the contest into a referendum on himself, he cannot credibly distance his persona from the defeat. Democrats seized the Senate and the House; Republicans lost ground even in deep-red Texas, Kansas, Ohio, South Carolina, Iowa and Kentucky.

The final two years of this presidency risk being consumed by domestic trench warfare — Democratic investigations into corruption, fights over judicial and executive nominees, and possible renewed impeachment debate. For India and the wider world, the question is whether Congress will challenge tariffs, Iran policy and nuclear brinkmanship that have roiled oil markets and trade talks.

Conservative voices abroad and allies in West Asia watch whether escalation ends or merely pauses. Israel’s parallel political theatre is a sideshow next to Gulf supply routes that matter for Indian imports. New Delhi hopes for breathing room in bilateral trade-negotiation leverage and lower energy-volatility premiums — provided Democratic majorities use oversight powers constructively rather than as pure partisan theatre.

Clipped wings in Washington do not automatically stabilise the global economy. They do reopen space for diplomacy, multilateral bargaining and Indian trade policy to manoeuvre without riding every presidential tweet into crisis.
