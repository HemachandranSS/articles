SEBI’s own path for settlement

The market regulator’s new settlement rules get most things right but some tweaks are needed

CKG Nair, V Shunmugam

Former Director, NISM; Partner, MCQuBe  |  November 5, 2026

After the board approved replacing the 2018 framework on September 24, SEBI notified the Settlement Regulations, 2026 on October 6; they took effect November 7. Global precedents — from Meta to Binance — show settlement as standard exit ramp; India’s version now has clearer gates and formulas, with lingering design bends.

The gates shut

Willful defaulters, fraudulent borrowers and fugitive economic offenders remain entirely ineligible for settlement. Under the 2018 regulations, excluding them was discretionary; now it is mandatory.

Three things right

A single formula multiplies a base amount by factors for gravity, record and mitigating circumstances. SEBI may offer settlement before a show-cause notice. Disgorgement of wrongful gains is now separate from the settlement amount rather than folded into it twice.

Three bends

Penalties scale by applicant type — independent directors face higher multiples than operating companies; stock exchanges face still higher floors — creating entity bias. Linking fines to net worth rather than offence size may under-penalise large frauds at well-capitalised firms. Allowing companies to pay for executives blunts individual deterrence.

Three lessons

UK FCA, EU and US SEC practice suggests scaling to revenue where appropriate, scrutinising hardship claims, and requiring responsible individuals to pay from personal funds. The destination is proportionality: the bill should reach the person who actually broke the rule.
