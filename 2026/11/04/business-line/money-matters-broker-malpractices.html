Stern action needed to check broker malpractices

Editorial Desk

The Hindu Business Line  |  Wednesday, November 4, 2026

Trading in the NSE cash segment surged post-pandemic — turnover rose from about ₹90 lakh crore in FY20 to ₹260 lakh crore in FY26, largely on retail participation. An NSE circular after inspection of brokers and agents for October 2025–March 2026 suggests much of that increase may have been stoked by intermediaries flagrantly breaching regulations to chase growth.

Malpractices are serious enough to warrant penalties. Brokers are disregarding SEBI processes for client funds and securities: money moves between upstreaming and downstreaming client nodal accounts that must channel client money only to clearing corporations. Fully paid client securities sit in unpaid pledge accounts — breaching brokers’ fiduciary responsibility.

Authorised persons violate rules by receiving client money and securities and trading with clients’ IDs and passwords though they may not handle client assets. Brokers encourage excessive leverage via the margin trading facility (MTF) at 9–14% annual interest; the MTF book exceeds ₹1.5 lakh crore versus about ₹45,000 crore three years ago, often with insufficient margin and inaccurate exchange reporting — a systemic risk if markets fall sharply.

Exchanges are first-line regulators. Finding these lapses but responding with mild advice amounts to kid-glove treatment that emboldens violators. Exchanges should warn, levy stringent penalties, and cancel registration if malpractices persist.

Key Terminologies

  1. Client Nodal Account Rules (வாடிக்கை முனைக் கணக்கு)
  2. Authorised Persons and Client Assets (அங்கீகृत பிரதிநிதிகள்)
  3. Margin Trading Facility Book (விளிம்பு வர்த்தக புத்தகம்)
  4. Exchange First-Line Regulation (பரிவர்த்தனை முதல் கட்ட ஒழுங்குமுறை)
