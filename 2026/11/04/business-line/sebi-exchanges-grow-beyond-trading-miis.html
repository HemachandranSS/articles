SEBI’s proposal to let exchanges grow beyond trading

MIIs are sitting on a wealth of data that can be monetised. But there are risks that need to be averted.

P. Saravanan, A. Paul Williams

IIM Tiruchirappalli; Sernova Financial  |  November 4, 2026

SEBI’s consultation paper proposes letting exchanges and depositories undertake related activities without case-by-case approval. Today, market infrastructure institutions (MIIs) need permission for fund deployment beyond treasury investments. The reform could unlock analytics and adjacent businesses built on trading and settlement data — if conflicts and cyber risks are managed.

The automatic route would cover work related or incidental to core securities-market functions, and activities similar to existing MII business conducted through entities regulated by RBI, IRDAI, IFSCA or PFRDA. Investment would be capped at the lower of 5% of prior March net worth or regulatory minimum net worth; post-investment net worth must stay at least 1.5× the prescribed floor.

MIIs hold rich data on order flow and settlement — commercially valuable but sensitive because MIIs also regulate members. Selling analytics may clash with private data vendors who rely on the same raw feeds. Ring-fencing subsidiaries and cyber rules for MII-owned firms need finalisation before scale-up.

Suggestions include defining “related or incidental” precisely; publishing half-yearly summaries of non-core activities; offering commercial data products to all vendors on equal terms; and retaining SEBI’s power to withdraw automatic approval if conditions break. Comments close November 12, 2026.
