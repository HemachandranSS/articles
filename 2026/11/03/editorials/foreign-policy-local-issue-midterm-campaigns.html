Foreign policy is a local issue in midterm campaigns

Republicans argue price rises are temporary and worth the war with Iran; tariffs, they say, are bringing manufacturing and jobs to America — yet more Republicans now say Trump’s policies have made the economy worse.

Sriram Lakshman

WASHINGTON  |  November 3, 2026

Foreign policy is rarely absent from midterm campaigns, but in 2026 it reads like a battleground state kitchen-table issue — tariffs, the Iran war, and the U.S. relationship with Israel shape ads from Kansas to Michigan.

The Iran factor

Campaign spots in Iowa and elsewhere tie the conflict to diesel, gas and fertilizer costs — reframing manufacturing and jobs rhetoric around energy shocks. Republicans call sacrifices a necessary war; some candidates distance themselves from Donald Trump without direct attacks. Polling shows the share of Republicans saying Trump’s policies worsened the economy has nearly doubled this year.

Senate races invoke nuclear capabilities and Iran risk. In Michigan, Democratic primaries fracture over Gaza and aid to Israel — proof that global alignments now mobilise local coalitions.
