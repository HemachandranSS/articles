Irrigation helps in climate resilience but it’s not enough

Irrigation must be complemented with better soil management and cropping practices

Saurabh Bandyopadhyay, Laxmi Joshi, Charu Jain, Kushagra Thakral

NCAER, New Delhi  |  November 3, 2026

A National Council of Applied Economic Research study across 57 irrigation projects, 20 States, nearly 600 villages and about 10,000 households finds that access to water in command areas improves incomes and markets — but does not by itself deliver climate-smart agriculture.

Sixty-eight per cent of command-area households reported higher productivity and incomes, against 67% in non-command areas reporting stagnation or decline. Seventy-six per cent in command areas saw better input and output market access; migration was 34% versus 46% outside command areas; new local service opportunities were reported by 30.2% of command households compared with 17.4% elsewhere.

Soil and practices

Forty-three point nine per cent of command respondents reported improved soil quality, against 5.5% in non-command areas — yet resilient farming practices were adopted by 58.7% in command tracts versus 51.1% outside, and climate-smart practices by 49.4% versus 43.6%. Irrigation creates necessary conditions for climate resilience but is not a silver bullet.

Full value depends on soil management, crop diversification and resilient technologies. Without them, expanded irrigation can deepen groundwater stress and lock in unsustainable cropping patterns. Agriculture accounted for 13.72% of India’s greenhouse-gas emissions in 2020 (Fourth Biennial Update Report); mitigation and adaptation must move together.
