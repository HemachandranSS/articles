Nvidia initiates probe into its CEO amid reports China spied on him

Tech firm opens inquiry into reports that Jensen Huang was ‘the target of a foreign intelligence effort’; U.S. officials believe the woman Huang acknowledged having affair with was spying for China; Beijing dismisses claims as a smear campaign.

Agence France-Presse

NEW YORK  |  November 10, 2026

AI chip behemoth Nvidia was plunged into turmoil on Monday after its CEO Jensen Huang acknowledged having an affair, with reports saying U.S. officials believe the woman was spying for China. Beijing denied the claim but Nvidia’s board opened an inquiry into the reports that Mr. Huang was “the target of a foreign intelligence effort.”

Late last month, The New York Times reported that U.S. intelligence officials determined that Alice Huang, a Chinese executive linked to a Singapore-based data centre company, Megaspeed, had been funnelling information to Beijing. The executive, also known as Huang Le, developed a close relationship with the Nvidia chief, the Times said.

Nvidia’s board announced on Sunday that it had opened a probe “in light of recent reporting that company’s chief executive and co-founder has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.” Jensen Huang is a huge figure in the AI revolution and a close informal adviser to U.S. President Donald Trump, who has repeatedly spoken out against regulating the industry so as not to let China gain the upper hand in the AI race.

“I was unfaithful to my wife,” Mr. Huang said in a message to company employees. “My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry.”

The Times said Alice Huang’s relationship with the Nvidia chief could have given her insights into Mr. Trump’s thinking around AI. Mr. Trump was briefed on the findings about Mr. Huang’s relationship roughly a year ago and chose not to act on the information, the Times said.

China rejected the spying allegations, with Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun telling a regular briefing on Monday that “the narrative of a so-called Chinese spy is entirely baseless vilification and smearing.” The issue of AI is likely to arise during Mr. Trump’s planned talks with President Xi Jinping later this month at a summit in China. Nvidia shares dropped roughly 5% in pre-market trading early Monday.
