Midterm election results place U.S.-Israel relations under further strain

Stanly Johny

November 6, 2026

Midterm election results put Washington–Tel Aviv ties under fresh strains after Donald Trump said roughly $4 billion in annual military aid would halt, citing GOP political costs.

Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed defeated Mike Rogers—among critics of aid to Israel and the first Muslim U.S. Senator in this telling—while opposition to Israel’s war moves into the mainstream. Figures such as New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani, El-Sayed and Representative Ro Khanna illustrate the shift. Even in the MAGA movement, commentators ask whether Gaza operations are a domestic liability.

Israel’s leadership long assumed bipartisan support—a strategic constant now eroded. Military–tech cooperation may face deprioritising if Trump acts as a lame duck yet still wields veto power. Vice-President J.D. Vance blamed the war for defeats, suggesting less unconditional backing. Gaza’s enclave trauma fuels political churn once insulated from electoral insulated foreign-policy debate.
