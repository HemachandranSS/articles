Green gridlock

Opposition to pumped storage plants lacks basis

Editorial Desk

The Hindu Business Line  |  Friday, November 6, 2026

India is increasingly opting for renewable energy capacities — wind and solar — in its march towards decarbonisation of the economy and Net Zero by 2070. With that thrust comes recognition that the grid must handle intermittency. More energy storage alongside transmission is key to grid robustness.

Leading approaches include pumped storage plants (PSPs) and battery energy storage systems (BESS). India has about 7.5 GW operational PSP capacity and 15.8 GW under construction — modest against ~290 GW potential and a 2035 ambition of 91 GW.

Policy supports include waiver of inter-state transmission charges, budgetary backing for enabling infrastructure, and the Central Electricity Regulatory Commission’s proposal to let PSP developers access the high-price day-ahead market — steps to improve project economics. Yet protests delay clearance even after CEA approval and environmental go-aheads, as at Torrent Power’s 3,000 MW Saidongar-I project in Raigad, Maharashtra, over felling ~48,000 trees where the developer pledged double compensatory planting.

PSP benefits can outweigh local deforestation if compensatory afforestation and safeguards are enforced — akin to “right of way” fights that once hobbled wind. Between 1970 and 1990 India built roughly 2,600 large dams (about 130 a year), showing infrastructure and environment need not be binaries. Environmental impact assessments and public hearings must be rigorous; once a project clears them, politically motivated obstruction should not hold up assets essential to renewable integration.
