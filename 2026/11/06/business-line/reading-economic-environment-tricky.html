Reading the economic environment can be tricky

There is a subjective element in interpreting economic trends as evidenced in the MPC Minutes

Madan Sabnavis

Chief Economist, Bank of Baroda  |  November 6, 2026

When John Maynard Keynes observed that economies are driven by “animal spirits,” he warned that interpretation is never mechanical. The Monetary Policy Committee’s August and October decisions show how small forecast shifts reprice entire markets: growth projections rose from 6.7% to 7.1% while inflation forecasts moved from 5% to 5.2% even as global uncertainty looked broadly similar.

Market’s reading

Traders treated the MPC Minutes and RBI Monthly Bulletin hawkishly: bond yields spiked and swap curves priced repo hikes. The overnight indexed swap (OIS) market at times implied a 6.25% repo rate (75 basis points above prevailing levels); one-year quotes signalled even larger moves. Middle-East war risk coloured sentiment; T-bill auction withdrawals added noise.

Crude trajectories, kharif harvest quality and possible fuel-price resets by OMCs before the December 4 policy will shape the next MPC call. Reading the environment means separating data from market narrative — and remembering that the same Minutes can justify opposite trades on consecutive days.
