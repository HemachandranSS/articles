Israel Opposition makes progress in its move to oust PM Netanyahu

Eisenkot says he expects President to give him the first mandate to try to form a govt.; Opposition partner Liberman lifts his veto on demands that ultra-Orthodox Jews serve in military, opening doors to a once improbable alliance with community.

Agence France-Presse

JERUSALEM  |  November 7, 2026

Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot said he expects President Isaac Herzog to grant him the first mandate after the October 27 election, advancing efforts to oust Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

Protesters in Jerusalem called for a Jewish–Arab government amid a polarised public. Avigdor Liberman lifted his veto on ultra-Orthodox military service demands, giving precedence to a broad anti-Netanyahu front. “Trying to have everything risks getting nothing,” the hawkish former defence minister said, opening an improbable alliance with secular liberals and Israeli-Arab lists.

“Trying to have everything risks getting nothing.” — Avigdor Liberman

Diplomats watch whether such breakthroughs can yield stable governance—or stall on unresolved security and budget fights.
