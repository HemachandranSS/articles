Asian parties reject ‘unilateral actions’, call for respect for international law

Stanly Johny

SEOUL  |  November 7, 2026

The International Conference of Asian Political Parties (ICAPP) adopted the Seoul Declaration Friday, warning against unilateral actions and affirming state sovereignty and territorial integrity under the UN Charter.

Eighty-three parties from November 4–6 plenary sessions—hosted jointly by South Korea’s Democratic Party and Opposition People Power Party—debated Palestine, Iran and wider geopolitical tensions even though the text avoided naming specific wars.

The declaration stressed non-interference, peaceful dispute settlement and equal status of nations for peace and reconciliation. It warned that threats of force and selective observance of obligations erode confidence in the legal order.

ICAPP Standing Committee chair Chung Eui-yong, ex–Foreign Minister, told The Hindu parties can influence governments toward peaceful settlements though ICAPP is not a government mediation body: “What we can do is influence our governments.” (The correspondent is in Seoul at ICAPP’s invitation.)
