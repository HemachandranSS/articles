Young and fast

Editorial Desk

As F1’s youngest champion, Antonelli can attract a new generation of fans

Editorial  |  Motorsport

Andrea Kimi Antonelli etched his name in motorsport history by becoming the youngest driver to win a Formula One World Championship. The 20-year-old has secured his maiden title with four races to spare after finishing third at the Mexico Grand Prix on Sunday. The Italian’s feat is remarkable, for he has claimed the crown in his sophomore campaign, becoming only the fifth driver in history to do so in the first or second season. Mercedes signed Antonelli for its junior driver programme when he was just 12. Since then, the Italian’s step towards F1 has been meticulously managed. After acing the F4 championships, Antonelli skipped one level to transition to F2. And when the opportunity arose to fill a seat vacated by seven-time champion Lewis Hamilton, Mercedes boss Toto Wolff took a bold gamble by drafting in the youngster. Antonelli’s rookie campaign in 2025 was baptism by fire, and he was outclassed by teammate George Russell. For 2026, F1 ushered in drastic changes with new engine and aerodynamic regulations, dramatically altering the dynamics of the cars. Though conventional wisdom suggested that the experienced Russell would be the favourite, the Italian prodigy embraced the challenge astonishingly well. This was best exemplified by how he won five consecutive races starting from the second round of the season.

One of the hardest things in F1 is handling the nerves associated with protecting the lead, especially when it is the driver’s first attempt at a Championship bid. Antonelli, though, was incredibly consistent throughout the year, and hardly made any errors. Titles are often decided by how a driver minimises his losses on bad days, and Antonelli showed great temperament in this regard. At the same time, he was ruthless on the track and made decisive overtaking manoeuvres. The defining race was in Monza, Italy, in September where Antonelli charged from 19th on the grid to win his home Grand Prix, finishing ahead of Russell who started second. It was a statement of intent, and strengthened his grip on the trophy. Drivers often raise their games following a world title, more so when success comes early in their career. Considering the giant leap Antonelli made to become F1’s first champion to be born in the 21st century, he will undoubtedly attract a new generation of fans. The charm of the sport lies in its iconic driver rivalries such as Ayrton Senna versus Alain Prost, and Michael Schumacher versus Mika Hakkinen. With four-time champion Max Verstappen widely considered the gold standard of the present era, Antonelli could well be the perfect candidate to battle the Dutchman in F1’s next great rivalry.
