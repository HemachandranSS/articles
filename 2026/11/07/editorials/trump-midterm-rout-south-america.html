After Donald Trump’s midterm poll rout, South America breathes easy

Shobhan Saxena

SAO PAULO  |  November 7, 2026

A Democratic “blue wave” in U.S. midterm polls left Donald Trump a lame-duck leader, easing pressure on Latin American governments that feared his Monroe Doctrine-style agenda with right-wing acolytes.

In Asuncion, protests over frozen bank accounts coincided with turmoil around President Santiago Peña, a Trump and Javier Milei ally, who announced resignation amid impeachment talk. Paradoxically, Washington’s weakened president could become a jinx for South America’s hard right.

Growing optimism

Brazil’s Lula da Silva, Argentina’s Milei and Cristina Kirchner’s Peronist camp, Venezuela’s Nicolas Maduro and Colombia’s Abelardo de la Espriella navigate new space for diplomacy as Trump faces headwinds on tariffs and regional security messaging. The rout lets many capitals breathe easier—at least until the next U.S. election cycle reopens uncertainty.
