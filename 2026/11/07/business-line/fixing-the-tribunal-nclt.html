Fixing the tribunal

NCLT must be independent in every sense, and efficient

Editorial Desk

The Hindu Business Line  |  Saturday, November 7, 2026

The Supreme Court’s remark that the Corporate Affairs Ministry had ‘hijacked’ staff appointments at the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) underscores a basic governance failure: the Ministry that often appears as petitioner still administratively controls the forum that hears cases.

Clarifications that recruitment rests with the tribunal do not remove executive sway over headcount, tenure and pay. The Tribunals Reform Bill, 2026 followed last year’s Madras Bar Association vs Union of India ruling, yet NCLT remains outside the new framework even as NCLAT is brought in — a constitutional inconsistency the Court flagged.

Only 67 of 320 administrative posts are filled; 16 judicial and technical seats lie vacant while insolvency dockets balloon — 14,813 IBC cases pending at end-March 2026. Administrative and budgetary control should move out of the Ministry of Corporate Affairs entirely. Moving from multi-member to single-member benches could double disposal capacity; recruitment must target proven professionals aged 35–40 willing to build 20-year tribunal careers.

E-filing milestones in annual reports mask benches still demanding paper filings. NCLT must be a credible ease-of-doing-business institution — independent, adequately staffed and fast — or investment and resolution flows will bypass India’s courts altogether.
