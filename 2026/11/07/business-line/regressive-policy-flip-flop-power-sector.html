OPINION · POWER SECTOR

A regressive policy flip-flop in power sector

The Haryana govt’s move seeking reversal of distribution licence granted to EPPL doesn’t augur well for privatisation

Somit Dasgupta

Visiting Professor, ICRIER; former Member (Economic & Commercial), CEA  |  Views personal  |  November 7, 2026

Haryana’s grant of a parallel distribution licence to Eleven Power Private Ltd (EPPL) for Nuh and Gurugram alarmed incumbent DHBVN and the State government — though consumer choice via parallel licensing is not novel (Jharkhand 2006, Mumbai). The pending Electricity (Amendment) Bill, 2025 would codify wire access against wheeling charges.

EPPL projects ₹4,700 crore investment over five years; DHBVN argues nearer ₹11,000 crore. The Haryana Electricity Regulatory Commission must stand on independent technical assessment — EPPL’s net worth is about ₹95 crore against market capitalisation near ₹4,000 crore, while eligibility rules typically require three years’ existence; EPPL is roughly a year old.

Instead of appealing to the Appellate Tribunal for Electricity, the State issued a policy direction under Section 108 of the Electricity Act, 2003 asking HERC to reconsider — an executive override of regulatory adjudication that is bad in law and bad for investment signals. Courts have upheld distribution privatisation in Chandigarh and Dadra & Nagar Haveli; Haryana’s reverse gear is retrograde.

Privatisation and licensed competition remain vital to distribution losses, quality and investment. The government should defend regulatory independence, not unsettle a sector investors already view as politically volatile.
