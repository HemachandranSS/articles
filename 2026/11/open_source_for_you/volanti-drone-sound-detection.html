VolAnti uses sound analysis to identify drones

FOSS Bytes

Open Source For You  |  November 2026  |  Page 16

VolAnti is an open source drone detection project by Agam Rossen that uses sound analysis to identify drones without relying on their radio transmissions. Its publicly available resources include firmware, printed circuit board (PCB) manufacturing files, a bill of materials, enclosure designs, test audio, and a browser-based simulator. This approach also makes the project relevant to detecting fibre-optic FPV drones, which can evade conventional radio-frequency detection because their control signals travel through a physical fibre-optic link. The device only detects and reports potential drones; it does not jam signals or attempt to intercept them.

The project’s hardware is built around an ESP32-S3 microcontroller and four ICS-43434 digital MEMS microphones arranged in a cross. Their audio streams are synchronised and combined to improve the signal-to-noise ratio before analysis. The project includes a LoRa radio module for sharing alerts between nearby units, an e-paper display, a buzzer and vibration alerts. Power comes from a rechargeable 2,500mAh lithium-polymer battery, with approximately 18–22 hours of operation.

By publishing its firmware, design files, test resources and assembly instructions, VolAnti gives developers a foundation for reproducing the detector, investigating acoustic signal processing and adapting the implementation to different environments.
