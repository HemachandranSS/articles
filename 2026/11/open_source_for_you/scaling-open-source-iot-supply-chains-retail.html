Scaling Open Source IoT Across Supply Chains and Retail

A field guide to the AI-native tool stack, the discipline that outlasts it, and the AI-quantum wave behind it.

By Bala Kalavala

Open Source For You  |  November 2026  |  Pages 80–82

In boardroom discussions around enterprise digital transformation, IoT is frequently mischaracterised as a hardware engineering problem. Executive teams invest millions in connected sensors, RFIDs, and smart gateways, only to watch initiatives stall at the pilot phase. Failure rarely stems from physical silicon; it arises because legacy architectures rely on proprietary, closed ecosystems that trap operational data in siloed cloud platforms, leading to exorbitant ingress fees and severe vendor lock-in.

To unlock measurable business value, CXOs must pivot from treating IoT as a tactical sensor deployment to viewing it as a core enterprise data fabric. Modern open source software stacks provide the strategic foundation needed to unify real-time physical events with enterprise resource planning (ERP) engines.

By shifting processing to the edge and streaming structured events into open data lakehouses, retail, consumer packaged goods (CPG), and supply chain organisations can convert raw telemetry into immediate bottomline impact.

The ERP-to-edge data bridge

The primary business challenge in modern supply chains is the time lag between physical reality on the warehouse floor and transactional records in ERP platforms such as SAP or Oracle.

Traditional architectures attempt to bridge this gap by shipping raw, unrefined telemetry directly to cloud databases, overwhelming data pipelines, and generating unsustainable cloud compute bills. The open source edge architecture resolves this by filtering, aggregating, and analysing telemetry at the physical boundary before pushing actionable signals directly into enterprise core systems.

The edge is also the AI layer

Most IoT investment cases stop at visibility — knowing where a pallet is, whether a shelf is empty, whether a compressor is drifting out of spec. But every layer of this architecture is also infrastructure for applied AI and treating it that way changes the ROI case.

At the edge, WasmEdge and ONNX Runtime aren’t just lightweight containers — they’re inference engines. Running a compact anomaly-detection or forecasting model directly on a retail gateway or a refrigerated transport unit means that a shrink event or a temperature excursion is flagged in milliseconds, with no round-trip to a cloud GPU and no dependence on network uptime. This is what makes real-time loss prevention and cold-chain intervention possible at all — a cloud-only inference call would already be too late.

Further upstream, the open lakehouse isn’t just cheap storage. Apache Iceberg tables accumulating years of labelled telemetry — shrink events, temperature breaches, shelf-replenishment cycles. These become the training data for the next generation of models. Every open-format, schema-governed event this architecture captures today is a feature set a data science team can use tomorrow, without an ETL rebuild to get it out of a proprietary vendor silo.

The strategic implication for CXOs: an open source IoT stack isn’t a parallel investment to an enterprise AI strategy — it’s the data and inference substrate that AI strategy depends on. Architectures that lock telemetry into closed platforms don’t just pay a cloud tax; they forfeit the training data asset altogether.

High-value industry scenarios: Retail, CPG, and supply chain

Bridging this architectural gap translates directly into bottomline operational efficiency. When edge devices interact seamlessly with core transactional databases, businesses shift from reactive crisis management to proactive automated workflows. Across retail and supply chain ecosystems, two high-impact scenarios demonstrate this value in practice as shown below.

Figure 1 (Edge to ERP) shows the flow from physical edge and local inference (retail shelves / RFID gates → WasmEdge / ONNX Runtime → shrink / loss detection), through a real-time event streaming spine (EMQX ↔ Apache Kafka / NATS), into core enterprise integration (Apache Flink CEP, open lakehouse with Apache Iceberg / S3, ERP integration with SAP / Oracle SCM), and finally to business outcomes: dynamic reorder alerts, automated cold-chain audits, and real-time on-shelf availability.

The business problem The open source solution ERP integration and business value
Consumer packaged goods (CPG) and cold chain logistics Perishable goods (pharmaceuticals, dairy, fresh produce) suffer billions in annual shrinkage due to undetected refrigeration failures during transit. Legacy loggers only record temperature data retroactively upon delivery, making insurance claims difficult and leading to complete inventory write-offs. Constrained sensor nodes inside refrigerated transport units continuously stream temperature, humidity, and location over lightweight open source messaging brokers (EMQX/Mosquitto). Local micro-inference models — typically lightweight anomaly-detection models (e.g., isolation forests or compact LSTM-based forecasters) small enough to run on constrained edge hardware — running on edge gateways flag temperature drift anomalies in real time. Apache Flink correlates location streams with SAP supply chain management (SCM) purchase orders. If a cold-chain breach occurs, the system automatically halts the shipment, alerts nearby distribution hubs, and recalculates stock replenishment before store stockouts happen—reducing inventory waste by up to 35%.
Omnichannel retail and dynamic on-shelf availability Retailers face significant revenue leakage from ‘phantom inventory’ — situations where the ERP shows an item in stock, but it is misplaced in a backroom or stolen from the sales floor, leading to lost sales and poor customer experiences. Smart shelves and overhead RFID readers leverage open-source WebAssembly (Wasm) runtimes to process tag reads locally. Edge microservices reconcile shelf counts against local point-of-sale (POS) scans every minute without saturating WAN bandwidth. Validated stock movement events stream directly into Kafka and update warehouse management systems (WMS). Store associates receive real-time replenishment tasks on mobile devices only when high-margin items fall below critical shelf thresholds, increasing on-shelf availability while optimising labour allocation.

Architectural principles for C-suite leaders

Building an open source IoT architecture requires clear governance to ensure long-term stability, compliance, and ROI.

  • Own your data standards: Never store operational telemetry in proprietary vendor formats. Mandate open storage standards like Apache Parquet governed by open table formats like Apache Iceberg. This ensures your supply chain data remains fully queryable by modern AI models and analytical engines for years without migration costs.
  • Decouple edge from cloud: Design field systems to operate autonomously during network disconnects. Gateways must buffer transactions locally using store-and-forward patterns to prevent operational downtime during internet outages.
  • Focus on event-driven ERP actions: Avoid polling databases. Configure your IoT stream layer to emit business-level events (e.g., ShipmentDelayed, ThresholdBreached, ShelfDepleted) directly into enterprise service buses, triggering immediate automated workflows inside ERP systems.

The enterprise value of IoT does not lie in the hardware—it lies in the velocity and quality of the operational decisions it enables. By deploying a modular, open source stack from the edge to the open lakehouse, forward-thinking organisations eliminate expensive vendor lock-in, streamline supply chain operations, and build a resilient data infrastructure that turns real-time physical events into sustainable competitive advantage.
