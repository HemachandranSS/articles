Focus on building rural economic clusters

Connecting coops, FPOs and SHGs with finance, skills and infrastructure will enable local producers capture that value

By S Mahendra Dev & KK Tripathy

In his 80th Independence Day address, the Prime Minister gave a clarion call to recalibrate and harmonise national development paradigms, reenergising the foundational architecture required to achieve a Viksit Bharat by 2047. The declaration on Saptadhara (Seven Streams) strengthens the case for a bottom-up model of rural development.

India's development architecture has constantly evolved in the quest for greater efficiency, coherence and impact. The Government carried out restructuring of Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSSs) to rationalise and eliminate overlap and fragmentation, enhance financial efficiency, strengthen accountability and give States/UTs greater ownership in implementation.

An extensive rural development ecosystem exists, but its components do not always work together. A farmer may receive production support; a cooperative or Farmer Producer Organisation (FPO) may facilitate aggregation; an individual or a group entrepreneur may receive assistance for an enterprise; and a village may benefit from infrastructure — yet the missing link around the rural value chain is the connection between producers and institutions, institutions and infrastructure, enterprises and finance/skills, and production and processing/markets. The answer to sustainable community development lies in a simple distinction: a programme can support an activity; a rural economic cluster can build an economy around it.

India has programmes covering effectively every major dimension of rural economic life — agriculture, dairy, fisheries, horticulture, FPOs, Self-Help Groups (SHGs), Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs), credit, skills, infrastructure and entrepreneurship. These programmes remain fragmented. The challenge, therefore, is increasingly not the absence of programmatic interventions but that of an integrated ecosystem.

ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITIES
Clusters can provide a practical framework and bridge this gap by bringing together producer institutions, common infrastructure, finance, technology, skills and markets around specific economic opportunities. A dairy cluster, for instance, should extend beyond milk production to connect producers with breeding, feed, veterinary services, finance, processing, cold chains, branding and markets. A fisheries cluster should integrate production with credit, insurance, processing, logistics and market access. Cooperatives, FPOs and SHGs can serve as institutional anchors, while MSMEs, banks, universities and technology providers can complete the ecosystem.

“A programme can support an activity; a rural economic cluster can build an economy around it.”

A recent field visit to Puri and Ganjam districts, Odisha made one point clear: the districts suffer from divided opportunity, not lack thereof. A dairy farmer receives veterinary support from one agency, feed from another, procurement through a cooperative, credit from a bank and insurance elsewhere. No single ecosystem connects these interventions into a competitive value chain. Processing has the same disconnect: machinery has been installed with public support, but some units operate at very low capacity because raw materials, markets, branding, logistics and enterprise capabilities have not kept pace.

In other words, the programme may perform, the asset may exist, but the enterprise may still be missing. The imperative is to create the institutional and entrepreneurial ecosystem by connecting cooperatives, FPOs and SHGs with finance, skills, infrastructure, processing and markets to enable local producers and enterprises to capture that value.

The ambition should be to create rural economic growth clusters, not isolated production clusters. In such a model, cooperatives, FPOs, fisher collectives and SHGs become institutional anchors; aggregation creates scale; shared infrastructure enables value addition; finance and technology strengthen enterprise capability; and branding, logistics and digital markets connect producers to greater opportunities. In coastal Odisha, for instance, dairy, fisheries and horticulture could form the foundation of such integrated clusters, creating a pathway from local production to processing, enterprise creation and wider national and international markets.

Clusters can provide the institutional ecosystem needed to align skills with local opportunities, connect enterprises with finance and markets, and coordinate government, community institutions and private partners along a common development pathway from local production to global opportunity.
