Such leadership factors have also given India sharper strategic clarity, thereby helping it to move forward in a prioritized and planned manner. That has been key to initiatives like the Neighbourhood First policy, and it is also the reason that India has been able to overcome three decades of hesitation with the US and the West. Changing administrations there or a different mix of issues have not changed that fundamental calculation.

At the same time, it has taken care to ensure that its interests and intentions are messaged in appropriate forums on issues like the Indo-Pacific, critical technologies, Covid-related needs and, more recently, the Ukraine conflict. An outcome of this has been that India is today seen as standing strongly on its own feet in the multipolar world of politics.

In other domains, too, India is endeavouring increasingly to shape global debates rather than accept those developed by others. Whether it is climate action, terrorism or the digital infrastructure that will govern future transactions, this is becoming more obvious. Its own traditions, history and culture are now projected more firmly on the global stage, whether it is solar energy, disaster resilience, food security or wellness.

The image of the New India abroad is of a nation that is powerful, capable, authentic and participative. It is respected for the nationalist spirit that drives change but also for contributing to collective endeavours.

When it comes to the global economy and technology, there is a marked interest in understanding India better and engaging more deeply with it. Its identity as a political democracy, pluralistic society and market economy encourages the perception of a possible partner for trusted manufacturing.
